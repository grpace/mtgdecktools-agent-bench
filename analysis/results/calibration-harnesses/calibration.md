# Calibration of the Rating

Generated by `npx tsx packages/site/src/calibrate.ts --sims 200 --power-sims 0 --replicates 200` (2026-10-23, 28 s on 12 processes). Pure simulation with the production rating model (`packages/site/src/rating.ts`: Bradley–Terry, prior SD 200 Elo, play/draw term; reported interval = the wider of the bootstrap and Laplace intervals); no LLM runs. Every number is in `calibration.json`.

Two methods are compared on the same simulated data:

- **Before:** two-stage bootstrap (events, then games within each drawn event); a pair is ranked only when the two individual 95% ranges don't overlap.
- **Now:** cluster bootstrap (events with their games intact; a contributor's events once there are 5 or more contributor clusters) with the small-cluster correction, floored by the Laplace interval, which carries a contributor component (30 Elo × √Σ share²) and a set component (30 Elo × √Σ set share²); A is ranked above B when the 95% interval of the rating difference A − B excludes 0, within a connected group.

## What This Decides

- **Intervals:** event-cluster bootstrap (a contributor's events once there are 5+ contributor clusters) with the small-cluster correction √(G/(G−1)), floored by the Laplace interval, plus a contributor component of 30 Elo × √Σ share² and a set component of 30 Elo × √Σ set share². Coverage 96%–96% across the design scenarios (central 96%; ratings with 120+ games 95%), against 97% and 8% wider ranges before. The event bootstrap alone covers – without contributor effects but 93% in the central scenario and – with strong ones: that is what the two components fix.
- **Ranking:** A ahead of B only when the 95% interval of A − B excludes 0, within a connected group. In the central scenario 28% of pairs are ranked (against 13% by non-overlap), 1.1% of them in the wrong order (0.7%–1.1% across the design scenarios; the close field is the worst). Two identities 100 Elo apart at 4 × 2 Private Tables are told apart half the time after about – games each (– tables), and 80% of the time after about – (– tables) and 80% wasn't reached in 48 tables.
- **Stress tests:** harnesses pooled, strong 96%. These break an assumption on purpose and are not a reason to widen every range: a spread wider than the rating prior (SD 200) shrinks the strongest and weakest rows toward the middle (in the very wide spread by about – Elo, covered – of the time; the rows between them –); matchups and set effects the model has no term for widen the error without the ranges seeing it. The export's predictive check (LAUNCH-CHECKLIST F-1) is how real data would show either.
- **Games floor:** 30 games stays. At G = 30 the least-sampled ratings explain 0.63 of the true-strength variance in the central scenario; in a close field (skill SD 50) no small sample is informative, so read the rank range.
- **Contributor floor:** a rating is provisional until 2 or more contributors supplied its games and none supplied more than half. With exactly two contributors that means an even split; in practice a firm rating needs three or more.

## Generative model

Each simulated community has K seat identities with true strengths θ ~ N(0, skill SD) and Zipf popularity, and C contributors with Zipf activity. Each contributor's seats of an identity play at θ + δ, δ ~ N(0, contributor SD), for every table they run: the contributor's harness configuration and setup. Every table seats 8 agents and plays a 3-round Swiss, best of 3, paired by the Director's own code (`pairSwissRound`, pairing version 4): round 1 random, then by match points with no rematches, a seat floated to the next record group rather than pair two seats of one identity before the last round, and the last round strictly by points; the loser of a game chooses to play first. Only game 1 of a match carries the play/draw side the fit uses (games 2–3 are the loser's choice, as in production). A Private Table is one contributor's 4 identities × 2 seats; a lobby table is 3–4 contributors with 1–3 seats each. Every seat draws a deck effect ~ N(0, deck SD) logits for the event, and each identity has a set-specific offset ~ N(0, set SD) Elo in one of 8 sets.

Forfeits count as losses: each contributor's setup of an identity goes silent in 0.5% of its games, or (10% of setups, flaky) 4%; a silent seat loses the game and stays silent for the match's next game half the time. Each game is lost to infrastructure with a 0.5% chance; the match then ends with no result (no match points, the pair has met) and its finished games stay rated. Ratings are fitted after 6, 12, 24, 48 tables, over the largest connected group, and compared with the truth shifted to the same mean (in the drift scenario, each identity's mean strength over the tables fitted).

Pairing check, over every simulated table: 345,600 matches, 7.1% of them between two seats of one identity (unrated), 1.2% ended with no result.

## Contributors

Rows with at least 30 games (now). "Floor fails": fewer than 2 contributors, or one above half of its games (the row stays provisional). "Range flag": leaving one contributor out moves the rating outside its 95% range. "Inconsistent": the shift without one contributor is at least 3.5 of its own standard errors (|z| ≥ 3.5). Both flags are counted on honest rows (the sandbagged row excluded), so they are false-alarm rates.
