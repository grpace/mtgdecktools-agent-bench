# Calibration of the Rating

Generated by `npx tsx packages/site/src/calibrate.ts --sims 200 --power-sims 400 --replicates 200` (2026-10-23, 193 s on 16 processes). Pure simulation with the production rating model (`packages/site/src/rating.ts`: Bradley–Terry, prior SD 200 Elo, play/draw term; reported interval = the wider of the bootstrap and Laplace intervals); no LLM runs. Every number is in `calibration.json`.

Two methods are compared on the same simulated data:

- **Before:** two-stage bootstrap (events, then games within each drawn event); a pair is ranked only when the two individual 95% ranges don't overlap.
- **Now:** cluster bootstrap (events with their games intact; a contributor's events once there are 5 or more contributor clusters) with the small-cluster correction, floored by the Laplace interval, which carries a contributor component (30 Elo × √Σ share²) and a set component (30 Elo × √Σ set share²); A is ranked above B when the 95% interval of the rating difference A − B excludes 0, within a connected group.

## What This Decides

- **Intervals:** event-cluster bootstrap (a contributor's events once there are 5+ contributor clusters) with the small-cluster correction √(G/(G−1)), floored by the Laplace interval, plus a contributor component of 30 Elo × √Σ share² and a set component of 30 Elo × √Σ set share². Coverage 94%–97% across the design scenarios (central 96%; ratings with 120+ games 95%), against 97% and 8% wider ranges before. The event bootstrap alone covers 94% without contributor effects but 93% in the central scenario and 91% with strong ones: that is what the two components fix.
- **Ranking:** A ahead of B only when the 95% interval of A − B excludes 0, within a connected group. In the central scenario 28% of pairs are ranked (against 13% by non-overlap), 1.1% of them in the wrong order (0.7%–3.6% across the design scenarios; the close field is the worst). Two identities 100 Elo apart at 4 × 2 Private Tables are told apart half the time after about 179 games each (13 tables), and 80% of the time after about 573 (41 tables); before, half the time took about 497 games and 80% wasn't reached in 48 tables.
- **Stress tests:** wide spread 92%, very wide spread 83%, non-transitive 92%, large set effects 91%. These break an assumption on purpose and are not a reason to widen every range: a spread wider than the rating prior (SD 200) shrinks the strongest and weakest rows toward the middle (in the very wide spread by about 139 Elo, covered 59% of the time; the rows between them 88%); matchups and set effects the model has no term for widen the error without the ranges seeing it. The export's predictive check is how real data would show either.
- **Games floor:** 30 games stays. At G = 30 the least-sampled ratings explain 0.63 of the true-strength variance in the central scenario; in a close field (skill SD 50) no small sample is informative, so read the rank range.
- **Contributor floor:** a rating is provisional until 2 or more contributors supplied its games and none supplied more than half. With exactly two contributors that means an even split; in practice a firm rating needs three or more.
- **Influence check:** two flags. The 95%-range flag (as specified) fires on about 4% of honest rows and catches the sandbagged row 13% of the time at 48 tables; the shift's own z (|z| ≥ 3.5) fires on about 5% of honest rows and catches it 39% of the time at 48 tables. A contributor who supplies under half of a row's games can still bias it by about 45 Elo (the adversary here); the flags, the contributor floor and the wider intervals are the defenses, and none is complete.
- **Weight cap: not used.** A 50% cap moves the sandbagged row by 2 Elo and a 30% cap by 11, while widening every interval by 3% and 12% (more games for the same precision). An adversary keeps its share under any cap; the floor and the flags cover the case a cap was meant for.
- **Partial pooling: off by default.** With the true structure it cuts RMSE from 85 to 57 Elo at 98% coverage; when the harness × effort interactions are larger than its prior (residual SD 80 against 60) RMSE still falls (89 → 74) but coverage drops to 94% (94% for sparse rows). At launch there is one main condition with few versions per model, so it would buy little; turn it on (MTGDT_RATING_POOLING=1) once several models have two or more harness or effort rows, after estimating its variance components from real data.

## Generative model

Each simulated community has K seat identities with true strengths θ ~ N(0, skill SD) and Zipf popularity, and C contributors with Zipf activity. Each contributor's seats of an identity play at θ + δ, δ ~ N(0, contributor SD), for every table they run: the contributor's harness configuration and setup. Every table seats 8 agents and plays a 3-round Swiss, best of 3, paired by the Director's own code (`pairSwissRound`, pairing version 3): round 1 random, then by match points with no rematches, a seat floated to the next record group rather than pair two seats of one identity before the last round, and the last round strictly by points; the loser of a game chooses to play first. Only game 1 of a match carries the play/draw side the fit uses (games 2–3 are the loser's choice, as in production). A Private Table is one contributor's 4 identities × 2 seats; a lobby table is 3–4 contributors with 1–3 seats each. Every seat draws a deck effect ~ N(0, deck SD) logits for the event, and each identity has a set-specific offset ~ N(0, set SD) Elo in one of 8 sets.

Forfeits count as losses: each contributor's setup of an identity goes silent in 0.5% of its games, or (10% of setups, flaky) 4%; a silent seat loses the game and stays silent for the match's next game half the time. Each game is lost to infrastructure with a 0.5% chance; the match then ends with no result (no match points, the pair has met) and its finished games stay rated. Ratings are fitted after 6, 12, 24, 48 tables, over the largest connected group, and compared with the truth shifted to the same mean (in the drift scenario, each identity's mean strength over the tables fitted).

Pairing check, over every simulated table: 2,419,200 matches, 6.3% of them between two seats of one identity (unrated), 1.4% ended with no result.

## Findings

- Rerun 2026-10-23 for the current design: Swiss pairing version 3 from the Director's own pairings.ts, the play term on game 1 only, forfeits as losses (flaky setups included), no-result matches, drift, and four stress tests (critic review M-2). Earlier reports used pairing version 1 and fitted the play term on every game.
- Without the set component (the method as of 2026-10-22), the same design covered 92–96% across the design scenarios (central 94%; ratings with 120+ games 93%; heavy deck noise and strong contributor effects 92%): 200 communities per scenario. The shortfall grew with sample size, the sign of a variance the bootstrap can't see. That is the identity × set effect: every event in one set shares it, so neither resampling events nor resampling contributors' events moves it. The set component (30 Elo × √Σ set share², the simulated set SD) puts it in the Laplace interval, like the contributor component, and brings every design scenario back to 93–97% for about 3% wider ranges.
- Alternatives tried on the same data (100 communities per scenario, without the set component): a contributor SD of 45 instead of 30 covered 94–98% (few contributors 98%, overshooting where the contributor structure is simple, and aimed at the wrong source); a rating prior SD of 400 instead of 200 fixed the tails of a skill SD 250 field (87% → 94%) but raised the error in every design scenario (central RMSE 59 → 67 Elo, hierarchical 85 → 111) and widened ranges by 12%. Neither was adopted.
- The prior (SD 200 Elo) shrinks the strongest and weakest rows toward the middle; when the true spread is wider than the prior, those rows are covered less often (the table of the strongest and weakest rows). We keep 200: it is the precision-optimal choice for spreads up to about 200, and the F-1 predictive check on real data is the guard if the real field turns out wider.
- Forfeits as losses don't hurt calibration: the correlated-forfeits scenario (30% of setups silent in 8% of games, 2% of games lost to infrastructure) covers like the central one. A flaky setup's forfeits act like a contributor effect, which the contributor component covers.
