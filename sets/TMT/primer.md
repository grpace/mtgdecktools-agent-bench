# MTG Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles (TMT) - Limited Deckbuilding Context

## 1. Format Overview and Supported Archetypes

This deck is being built for the **TMT** limited format.

Use the following archetype descriptions as your primary set-specific guidance when building and tuning a 40-card deck. Prioritize cards that clearly align with one of these archetype plans.

### 1.0. Bomb-First Evaluation
* **Evaluate Rares/Mythics First:** Start by laying out your rares and mythics before locking your final build direction.
* **Include or Build Around Them When Castable:** If a rare or mythic is realistically castable with your mana base, prioritize including it and shaping your deck to maximize its impact.
* **Synergy Supports Card Quality:** TMT rewards synergy, but when choices are close, lean toward the higher-impact rare/mythic and then fit your archetype shell around it.

### 1.1. Format Identity
* **Five Supported Two-Color Lanes:** Most successful decks are built around one of five clearly supported archetypes: WB Sneak, UR Artifacts, BG Disappear, RW Alliance, and UG Mutagen. All five archetypes are fairly well supported and relatively balanced.
* **Synergy + Interaction Format:** Synergy matters, but games are still decided by removing key payoffs and creating profitable attacks. Unsupported color pairs usually need multiple bombs or powerful gold cards to compete, though hybrid cards and overlapping themes (Mutagen, artifacts, Food) make some allied-color builds viable when your pool naturally supports them.
* **Board Presence Matters:** Many engines rely on creatures entering, leaving, or connecting in combat, so creature count and curve discipline remain foundational. Decks that fall behind on board often struggle to leverage their mechanics.
* **High Legendary Density:** The set has a high density of legendary Turtles and other named characters, so most decks will have access to multiple legends. Curve and mana bases should assume you will often play several legendary creatures at once; avoid overloading on expensive legends that compete for the same turn.
* **Powerful Commons and Uncommons:** The set is relatively powerful at common and uncommon compared to typical limited environments. Cards matter—there are few true junkers, and most cards have a home. This also means weak cards like [[Foot Mystic]] or [[Putrid Pals]] feel especially bad when opponents are casting premium commons and uncommons.
* **Wide Strategic Range:** The format supports aggressive decks, grindy decks, good-stuff builds, and highly synergistic builds. Mutagen tokens provide a modular way to spend mana and spread stats; the artifact deck in particular rewards careful sequencing and planning.

### 1.2. Macro Deckbuilding Rules
* **Start with Plan, Then Fill Curve:** First lock in your synergy package, then ensure your deck can cast meaningful spells on turns 2-5.
* **Play Enough Enablers:** Build around payoff-to-enabler pairs (for example, Disappear payoffs plus token/sacrifice outlets).
* **Respect Combat:** Sneak and Alliance punish weak board states. Decks that cannot block early often lose to burst damage turns.
* **Prioritize Immediate Impact:** Cards that produce board presence plus material (token, artifact, or counter) outperform isolated rate creatures in synergy mirrors.

## 2. Key Mechanics and Deckbuilding Implications

### 2.1. Mutagen Tokens
* **Rules Text Impact:** Mutagen tokens are artifact tokens with "{1}, {T}, Sacrifice: Put a +1/+1 counter on target creature. Activate only as a sorcery."
* **Deckbuilding Impact:** They are both board-growth tools and artifact enablers, which lets UG Mutagen and UR Artifacts overlap naturally. Unlike draw-based tokens (such as "map" style effects), Mutagen never gives you extra cards—treat it as repeatable stat investment, not card advantage, and make sure your deck has other ways to pull ahead on cards if you plan for long games.
* **Play Pattern:** Mutagen is strongest when your deck has cards that care about counters ([[Michelangelo, Mutant BFF]], [[Michelangelo, Weirdness to 11]], [[Mikey & Leo, Chaos & Order]]) or artifacts ([[Donatello, Way with Machines]], [[Ravenous Robots]], [[Technodrome]]). In aggressive shells, prioritize using Mutagen to push immediate combat damage; in slower shells, bank them until you can safely grow a key threat out of range of common removal.

### 2.2. Sneak
* **Rules Text Impact:** Alternative cost requiring you to return an unblocked attacker during the declare blockers step.
* **Deckbuilding Impact:** Sneak decks need evasive/low-cost attackers that can reliably become unblocked, such as [[Featherbrained Filcher]], [[April O'Neil, Kunoichi Trainee]], [[Squirrelanoids]], and [[Dream Beavers]]. Returning an expensive creature to hand is a real cost; prioritize attackers that are cheap or have strong ETB/death triggers.
* **Play Pattern:** Sneak dramatically increases burst damage and ETB quality; include enough cheap attackers so Sneak cards (like [[Oroku Saki, Shredder Rising]], [[Leonardo, Cutting Edge]], [[Karai, Future of the Foot]], or [[Donatello's Technique]]) are cast for their alternate cost consistently instead of at full retail. Because Sneak can only be used in the declare blockers step after blockers are declared, leaving multiple attackers unblocked is especially dangerous against WB decks.
* **Timing Discipline:** Sneak is an alternative way to cast the spell (not an activated ability), so it can be countered like any other spell. Build with cheap interaction to clear blockers before declare blockers, and as the defending player avoid casting removal during declare blockers unless necessary—opponents can respond by Sneaking their creature back to hand, saving it and still getting the new threat.
* **Unblockable Support:** Lands like [[Escape Tunnel]] and [[Turtle Lair]] that can make small creatures effectively unblockable are strong Sneak enablers and should be valued more highly in dedicated Sneak shells.

### 2.3. Disappear
* **Rules Text Impact:** End-step check for whether a permanent left the battlefield under your control this turn.
* **Deckbuilding Impact:** Requires repeatable ways to sacrifice, cash in Food, or otherwise move permanents off board (for example, [[Ice Cream Kitty]], [[Anchovy & Banana Pizza]], [[Guac & Marshmallow Pizza]], [[Escape Tunnel]], or blink effects like [[Go Ninja Go]] and [[Don & Leo, Problem Solvers]]).
* **Play Pattern:** Try to trigger Disappear the same turn your payoff enters to guarantee immediate value; sequencing a sacrifice/Food activation before you cast [[Foot Mystic]], [[Michelangelo, Game Master]], or [[Rat King, Verminister]] is ideal. In combat, remember that trading blockers also turns on opposing Disappear triggers—high-toughness blockers that can absorb attacks without dying (like [[Primordial Pachyderm]] or a grown [[EPF Point Squad]]) are particularly valuable.
* **Consistency Check:** If your deck cannot trigger Disappear cheaply and often, trim clunky Disappear payoffs and pivot toward cleaner midrange interaction.

### 2.4. Alliance
* **Rules Text Impact:** Triggers whenever another creature you control enters.
* **Deckbuilding Impact:** Tokens and low-curve creatures become premium because each ETB can represent damage or counters.
* **Play Pattern:** Alliance decks want to spend mana each turn adding bodies, not holding up expensive reactive spells. Many payoffs (like [[EPF Point Squad]], [[Mutant Town Musicians]], or [[Raphael, Tough Turtle]]) effectively turn "go wide" into "go tall," so leaning into a single, repeatedly-buffed threat backed by a token field is often stronger than trying to spread counters thinly across your whole team.
* **Burst Enablers Matter:** Prioritize cards that create multiple ETBs in one turn, especially [[Mechanized Ninja Cavalry]] and [[The Last Ronin's Technique]].

## 3. Archetype Lanes

### 3.1. WB Sneak (Sneaky Ninjas)
* **Core Theme:** Sneak pressure with evasive attackers and high-impact combat steps. One of the better decks in the format, with the highest potential for failing when built incorrectly.
* **Game Plan:** Be explosive and get under the opponent. Ideally play an evasive one-drop into a two-mana Sneak card. Force through unblocked attacks with cheap evasive bodies, convert those attacks into Sneak turns, and snowball through ETB/combat-damage triggers and graveyard loops. Most games are decided by one or two huge Sneak turns where you upgrade a small attacker into a much larger threat.
* **One-Drop Priority (Critical):** The difference between a great WB Sneak deck and a middling one is having enough one-drops. Target four to five one-drops. Best: [[Dream Beavers]] (premium uncommon—evasive, drain, scry to find enablers or payoffs). Also strong: [[Leonardo, Leader in Blue]] (pseudo-evasive via first-strike threat), [[Featherbrained Filcher]], [[Squirrelanoids]].
* **Two-Drop Enablers:** [[April O'Neil, Kunoichi Trainee]] (scry 2, can't be blocked by power 3+), [[Mechanized Ninja Cavalry]] (two bodies—wider board makes sneaking easier). When facing [[Mechanized Ninja Cavalry]], block the original card, not the token, to force Sneak decks to pick up the token instead of the card.
* **Sneak Payoff Count:** Target seven to eight Sneak cards as payoffs. Best common: [[Oroku Saki, Shredder Rising]] (cheap Sneak cost, draws on damage). [[Foot Ninjas]] (five hasty damage) is strong but fails without enablers—as a 6-mana 5/5 it is poor. Target roughly eight cards costing less than three that help with sneaking.
* **Key Cards:** [[Dream Beavers]], [[Splinter, Hamato Yoshi]] (enabler and payoff—cheap Sneak cost, pumps other Ninjas), [[Karai, Future of the Foot]], [[Karai's Technique]], [[Oroku Saki, Shredder Rising]], [[Foot Ninjas]], [[Leonardo, Leader in Blue]], [[The Last Ronin's Technique]], [[Koya, Death from Above]] (also splashable in BG Food), [[Quintessential Katana]], [[Hamato Guardian Stance]].
* **Quintessential Katana:** Single-mana equipment that auto-attaches when a Ninja enters. In Ninja-heavy decks you rarely pay the equip cost. Gives +1/+1, lifelink on combat damage, and untaps the creature—effectively vigilance. Solves the inherent ninja weakness of being poor on defense; the life gain can be substantial.
* **Legendary Rule:** For fine-to-medium legends, play two copies; for good ones, three. Cheap legends are more likely to trade off or eat removal, reducing legend-rule conflicts.
* **Deckbuilding Priority:** Evasion, cheap interaction, and Sneak payoffs over clunky standalone creatures. Your curve should be dense at one to three mana so you can attack early, Sneak in higher-impact threats, then redeploy the bounced creature for additional ETB value.
* **Cross-Lane Overlap:** Returning attackers for Sneak also satisfies Disappear; WB Sneak can lean into BG-style engines like [[Ninja Teen]] or [[Super Shredder]] if your pool supports them, and cards like [[Escape Tunnel]] that set up unblockable attacks are premium in this lane.

### 3.2. UR Artifacts (Machines)
* **Core Theme:** Artifact density plus artifact payoff creatures/spells. Centered around [[Nobody]] for value loops. This deck is happiest to be built without rares or mythics—it has a strong common/uncommon shell.
* **Game Plan:** Curve cheap, "trinkety" artifacts into scaling payoffs, then win through evasive artifact threats and recursive value loops. Use [[Nobody]] to pick up artifacts with ETB value and replay them. Aim for roughly 10–14 artifacts in a dedicated build so your engines are reliably active, and expect many games to go long as you reuse ETB artifacts.
* **Nobody + Value Artifacts:** Play two to three copies of [[Nobody]]. Pick up [[Mouser Foundry]] (makes token on enter and leave), [[Omni-Cheese Pizza]] (draws on enter), [[Mechanized Ninja Cavalry]], or [[Spicy Oatmeal Pizza]] (reuse removal) for repeated value. [[Nobody]] scries even if you don't pick something up.
* **Classic One-Two:** [[Mouser Foundry]] on turn two (make token), [[Nobody]] on turn three (pick up Foundry, make token again, scry). Replay Foundry later for more tokens.
* **Premium Uncommons:** [[Metalhead]] (bounce your own artifacts for value or tempo, can grow with sacrifice), [[Casey Jones, Jury-Rig Justiciar]] (artifact tutor on ETB), [[Baxter Stockman]] (token + combat pump), [[Brilliance Unleashed]] (deal 5 or reanimate artifact—e.g., [[Omni-Cheese Pizza]] becomes kill + 3/3 flyer that draws).
* **Chrome Dome:** Rare that pumps artifact creatures and can copy any artifact for five mana (token gains haste, sacs next end step). Copy [[Turtle Blimp]], [[Spicy Oatmeal Pizza]], or [[Omni-Cheese Pizza]] for value. Strong first-pick for artifact decks.
* **Removal with Upside:** [[Ooze Spill]] (counter + Mutagen) and [[Return to the Sewers]] (tuck + Mutagen) are premium—counter/tuck spells that give material are often underrated. [[Return to the Sewers]] is one of the best commons in the set.
* **Turtle Blimp:** Strong generically—vehicle that self-crews with a 2/2 token. Excellent target for [[Brilliance Unleashed]] (get 3/3 flyer + 2/2 + kill a creature).
* **Key Cards:** [[Nobody]], [[Donatello, Turtle Techie]], [[Mouser Foundry]], [[Omni-Cheese Pizza]], [[Donatello, Way with Machines]], [[Baxter Stockman]], [[Ravenous Robots]], [[Chrome Dome]], [[Technodrome]], [[Mouser Mark III]], [[Buzz Bots]], [[Henchbots]], [[Casey Jones, Jury-Rig Justiciar]], [[Metalhead]], [[Sewer-veillance Cam]], [[Brilliance Unleashed]], [[Ooze Spill]], [[Return to the Sewers]], [[Turtle Blimp]].
* **Deckbuilding Priority:** Maintain high artifact count so payoff cards are consistently turned on; artifacts that bring extra artifacts (tokens or Food/Mutagen), like [[Mechanized Ninja Cavalry]], [[Mouser Foundry]], [[Crustacean Commando]], and [[Slithering Cryptid]], are premium glue that let you cross into UG Mutagen. Cheap artifacts that replace themselves (like [[Omni-Cheese Pizza]] and [[Sewer-veillance Cam]]) are especially strong because you can pick them up with [[Nobody]] for repeated value.

### 3.3. BG Disappear
* **Core Theme:** Triggering Disappear by having your permanents leave the battlefield. Green decks (BG and UG) are often "good stuff" decks—synergies exist but are not at the core. You're casting good expensive cards and likely splashing.
* **Game Plan:** Use Food/tokens/sacrifice patterns to trigger end-step value repeatedly, growing your board and draining resources while grinding out long games. Many of your best draws look like "play a Disappear payoff, then immediately cash in a Food or token" so you get value right away.
* **Omni-Cheese Pizza for Disappear:** One of the better ways to trigger Disappear the turn your payoff enters. Sacrificing Food normally costs mana; [[Omni-Cheese Pizza]] filters mana (net neutral) so you can cast a Disappear card and trigger it same turn without needing seven mana.
* **Anchovy & Banana Pizza:** When it enters, you must destroy target creature. If the opponent removes their only creature in response, you must target one of your own. Be aware of this templating.
* **Key Cards:** [[Michelangelo, Game Master]], [[Lord Dregg, Insect Invader]], [[Rat King, Verminister]], [[Pizza Face, Gastromancer]], [[West Wind Avatar]], [[Super Shredder]], [[Ice Cream Kitty]], [[Anchovy & Banana Pizza]], [[Guac & Marshmallow Pizza]], [[Spicy Oatmeal Pizza]], [[Courier of Comestibles]], [[Tunnel Rats]], [[Omni-Cheese Pizza]], and flexible removal like [[Tainted Treats]].
* **Deckbuilding Priority:** Disappear payoffs are only as good as your reliable leave-the-battlefield enablers; prioritize low-opportunity triggers (Food activations, sacrifice outlets, blink/bounce lands like [[Northampton Farm]] and [[Escape Tunnel]]) over expensive setup. Many Sneak cards incidentally enable Disappear, making WB/BG mixes viable when your fixing cooperates, and multicolor Food payoffs like [[Everything Pizza]] can be worth splashing when you already want multiple colors and Food density.

### 3.4. RW Alliance
* **Core Theme:** Repeated creature-entry triggers that convert into damage, counters, or pressure. Well supported with equally good payoffs and ways to go wide.
* **Game Plan:** Go wide, keep pressure high, and chain creature ETBs for compounding damage and counters. Your best turns double-spell cheap creatures or token makers to trigger multiple Alliance effects in a single combat step.
* **Mighty Mutanimals:** One of the top cards in the set. Enters as 5/4 worth of stats across two bodies, triggers Alliance twice immediately, and puts a counter on a creature every time another creature enters. If it sticks, you win easily. Prioritize highly.
* **Mechanized Ninja Cavalry:** The most important common to this deck. Play it on two every time; aim for three to five copies. It is the best cheap token maker and makes all Alliance cards pop off. Take it early (second, third, fourth pick).
* **Token Makers:** [[The Last Ronin's Technique]] (three tokens, can Sneak for tapped-and-attacking), [[Jennika, Bad Apple Big Sister]] (land cycler—best of the land cyclers for alliance synergy), [[Old Hob, Alleycat Blues]] (two bodies the turn it comes down; use {1}{W} to give the token indestructible so it survives past end step).
* **Best Uncommon Payoff:** [[Lita, Little Orphan Amphibian]]—pile counters on her as soon as possible. She becomes a giant snowballing threat.
* **Other Payoffs:** [[Leonardo, Leader in Blue]] (pumps team when Sneaked), [[The Neutrinos]] (blinks a creature to attack for haste, triggers its own Alliance).
* **EPF Point Squad:** Strong with many token makers (can grow to four or six power quickly); weak if you only get one counter per turn. Skip if you lack good enablers.
* **Go Ninja Go:** Hit or miss—excellent with a strong board (blink + removal), but weak with lots of 1/1s.
* **Key Cards:** [[Mighty Mutanimals]], [[Mechanized Ninja Cavalry]], [[Lita, Little Orphan Amphibian]], [[Raphael, Tough Turtle]], [[EPF Point Squad]], [[Mutant Town Musicians]], [[Wingnut, Bat on the Belfry]], [[The Neutrinos]], [[Old Hob, Alleycat Blues]], [[Raph & Leo, Sibling Rivals]], [[The Last Ronin's Technique]].
* **Deckbuilding Priority:** Cheap creatures and token makers are premium because they trigger Alliance repeatedly; avoid loading up on expensive noncreature spells that do not add bodies to the board. [[Mighty Mutanimals]] in particular plays like a rare-level engine—it's excellent in RW Alliance but also strong enough to run in any white creature deck because it provides immediate stats and ongoing counter generation.

### 3.5. UG Mutagen
* **Core Theme:** Mutagen token production and +1/+1 counter conversion. Green decks often play as "splashy multicolor good stuff" with green as the base. Fixing is good; expect to play some mix of three colors.
* **Game Plan:** Play to the board early, then snowball by turning Mutagen counters into dominant combat steps; this is more of a straightforward UG midrange beatdown deck than a slow ramp shell. Your creatures start modest and become difficult to block or race as Mutagen accumulates and counter payoffs come online. A true UG tempo/counter deck with multiple [[Genghis Frog]] exists but doesn't come together often.
* **Fixing:** [[Everything Pizza]] (land search + five-color sink), [[Escape Tunnel]], [[Frog Butler]], [[Mona Lisa, Science Geek]] (taps for X mana where X is power—put Mutagen on her to scale). [[Mona Lisa]] + [[Guac & Marshmallow Pizza]]: target Mona Lisa with the pizza to untap her, effectively turning the pizza into a ritual (e.g., turn four: tap Mona Lisa, cast pizza on her, she untaps as 3/3, cast a six-drop).
* **Courier of Comestibles:** One of the best uncommons. Toolbox—search for any Food: removal ([[Anchovy & Banana Pizza]]), trick ([[Guac & Marshmallow Pizza]]), fixing ([[Everything Pizza]], [[Omni-Cheese Pizza]]), or [[Pizza Face, Gastromancer]], [[Ice Cream Kitty]], etc.
* **Lessons from Life:** Excellent card draw plus optional land drop. Note: the land enters tapped—sometimes decline to avoid locking yourself out of casting a spell.
* **West Wind Avatar:** Prefer sacrificing tokens over lands when possible; you want lands in play to deploy drawn cards. Sack a land when empty-handed or when you have no other way to trigger Disappear.
* **Punk Frogs:** 4/5 with ward 3 is hard to remove; [[Anchovy & Banana Pizza]] costs seven mana to kill it.
* **Key Cards:** [[Genghis Frog]], [[Mutagen Man, Living Ooze]], [[Michelangelo, Weirdness to 11]], [[Michelangelo, Mutant BFF]], [[Mikey & Don, Party Planners]], [[Mikey & Leo, Chaos & Order]], [[Ray Fillet, Man Ray]], [[Crustacean Commando]], [[Slithering Cryptid]], [[Zoo Escapees]], [[Mutant Chain Reaction]], [[The Ooze]], [[Lessons from Life]], [[Courier of Comestibles]], [[West Wind Avatar]], [[Punk Frogs]], and crossover role-players like [[Transdimensional Bovine]] or [[Frog Butler]].
* **Deckbuilding Priority:** Prioritize cheap cards that both create Mutagen and reward you for putting counters on creatures; this lane naturally cross-synergizes with UR artifact payoffs because Mutagen tokens are artifacts and many enablers (like [[Crustacean Commando]] and [[Slithering Cryptid]]) are castable in either shell. Cards that make it hard to block countered creatures, like [[Michelangelo, Mutant BFF]], frequently turn a stable board into a lethal attack.

## 4. Practical Deckbuilding Guidance

* **Stay Archetype-Focused:** Maximize one primary plan instead of playing disconnected good-stuff cards.
* **Curve Still Matters:** Even synergy decks need enough 2- and 3-drops to enable Sneak, Alliance, and board development.
* **Support Payoffs with Enablers:** A payoff-heavy hand without enablers is a mulligan in disguise.
* **Removal Philosophy:** There is a lot of removal in the set. You want some, but don't take it very highly—removal tends to find you. Many creatures double as removal or interaction (e.g., [[Metalhead]]). [[Return to the Sewers]] wheels but shouldn't—it's one of the best commons. Counter/tuck spells that give material ([[Ooze Spill]], [[Return to the Sewers]]) are stronger than they look. Only prioritize removal early if it's premium; otherwise focus on your synergy shell and pick up removal when it comes.
* **Interaction Baseline:** Prioritize efficient removal across colors, especially [[Uneasy Alliance]], [[Grounded for Life]], [[Stomped by the Foot]], [[Manhole Missile]], [[Bot Bashing Time]], [[Return to the Sewers]], and [[Tenderize]].
* **Plan to Win Combat:** Synergy creates edges, but conversion happens through combat. Build for attack steps and blocks, not just value text.
* **Legend Rule Awareness:** Commons such as [[April O'Neil, Kunoichi Trainee]], [[Donatello, Turtle Techie]], and [[Stockman, Mad Fly-entist]] are legendary; avoid overloading your list with duplicate legends that clog hands.
* **White Pairing Note:** White often performs best when paired with Red's Alliance shell unless your White-Black pool has enough true Sneak enablers and payoffs.

1. **[[Grounded for Life]]** - Often cast for two mana against tapped attackers; clean answer to large threats.

1. **[[Return to the Sewers]]** - One of the best commons in the set. Tuck removal plus Mutagen token; tends to wheel but shouldn't. Counter/tuck spells that give material are premium.

3. **[[Crustacean Commando]]** - Defensive early play that supplies Mutagen and artifact synergy.

2. **[[Anchovy & Banana Pizza]]** - Full removal spell that also supports Food and sacrifice/disappear shells.

### 6.5. Green
1. **[[Primordial Pachyderm]]** - Excellent common stat package with reach and trample.
2. **[[Ragamuffin Raptor]]** - Strong value top-end that recovers key creatures or Food.

## 7. Archetype Build Guides

### 7.1. WB Sneak
* **Core Build:** Evasive two- and three-drops, cheap removal, and Sneak payoffs.
* **Priority Pairings:** [[Karai, Future of the Foot]] + ETB creatures; [[Koya, Death from Above]] + Sneak loops; [[Karai's Technique]] as burst swing.
* **Important Note:** This deck is strongest when your early creatures are useful both as pressure and as Sneak enablers; avoid expensive cards that do not impact Sneak turns.

### 7.2. UR Artifacts
* **Core Build:** 10+ artifacts and multiple artifact payoffs.
* **Priority Pairings:** [[Donatello, Way with Machines]] with token-generating artifacts, [[Baxter Stockman]] with evasive artifact bodies, [[Mouser Foundry]] with artifact ETB payoffs.
* **Top-End Plan:** [[Brilliance Unleashed]] is strongest when your graveyard has meaningful artifacts to return.

### 7.3. BG Disappear
* **Core Build:** Disappear payoffs plus reliable sacrifice outlets/token producers.
* **Priority Pairings:** [[Pizza Face, Gastromancer]] and [[Lord Dregg, Insect Invader]] with Food and disposable tokens; [[Ice Cream Kitty]] turns disposable resources into cards.
* **Top-End Plan:** [[West Wind Avatar]] stabilizes races while self-enabling Disappear.

### 7.4. RW Alliance
* **Core Build:** Low curve, token makers, and repeated ETB turns.
* **Priority Pairings:** [[The Neutrinos]] for blink-attack pressure, [[Old Hob, Alleycat Blues]] for recurring ETB triggers, [[Mighty Mutanimals]] for scaling counters, [[The Last Ronin's Technique]] for multi-trigger burst turns.
* **Important Rules Interaction:** The 2/2 token from [[Old Hob, Alleycat Blues]] can survive its own end-step destroy trigger if you activate Old Hob's "{1}{W}: Target attacking creature token gains indestructible until end of turn." and have the mana available.
* **Closing Pattern:** Alliance chip damage plus wide board attacks closes games quickly once synergies are online.

### 7.5. UG Mutagen
* **Core Build:** Mutagen production, counter payoffs, and card-advantage sinks.
* **Priority Pairings:** [[Genghis Frog]] and [[Crustacean Commando]] for token volume; [[Michelangelo, Mutant BFF]] for combat pressure; [[Ray Fillet, Man Ray]] for counter-to-card conversion.
* **Cross-Synergy Note:** Many mutagen cards are artifacts, so artifact-adjacent payoffs remain playable here.
* **Refuel Pattern:** [[Lessons from Life]] is an important bridge card that reloads while helping hit mana for large multi-spell turns.

## 9. Synergy Packages and Notable Combos

### 9.1. Nobody + Mouser Foundry (UR Artifacts)
* **Cards:** [[Nobody]] + [[Mouser Foundry]]
* **Why It Works:** Play Foundry on turn two (make token). Play Nobody on turn three, pick up Foundry (Foundry leaves, make token again; Nobody scries 1). Replay Foundry later for more tokens. Extends to [[Omni-Cheese Pizza]], [[Spicy Oatmeal Pizza]], [[Sewer-veillance Cam]].

### 9.2. Mona Lisa + Guac & Marshmallow Pizza (Green Ramp)
* **Cards:** [[Mona Lisa, Science Geek]] + [[Guac & Marshmallow Pizza]]
* **Why It Works:** Tap Mona Lisa for mana, cast the pizza targeting Mona Lisa—she gets +2/+2 and untaps. She's now a 3/3 for the turn. On turn four this can enable a six-drop. The pizza effectively becomes a ritual.

### 9.3. Boar/Rhino Unblockable Lock
* **Cards:** [[Bebop, Warthog Warrior]] + [[Rocksteady, Crash Courser]]
* **Why It Works:** Menace plus "can't be blocked by more than one creature" can make attacks functionally unblockable.

### 9.4. Tap-Then-Exile Interaction
* **Cards:** [[Utrom Scientists]] + [[Henchbots]]
* **Why It Works:** [[Utrom Scientists]] taps and stuns targets, enabling consistent [[Henchbots]] exile windows.

### 9.5. Fight-to-Grow Counter Engine
* **Cards:** [[Venus, Torn Between Worlds]] + [[Novel Nunchaku]]
* **Why It Works:** Fight damage grows [[Venus, Torn Between Worlds]], then counter-enabled combat damage can convert into cards.

### 9.6. Sneak Burst Finisher
* **Cards:** [[The Last Ronin's Technique]] + [[Leonardo, Leader in Blue]]
* **Why It Works:** Multiple tapped-and-attacking bodies plus team-wide power boost create lethal swing turns.

## 10. Mana Base and Splash Guidance

### 10.1. Primary Fixing Lands
* **Archetype Duals:** [[Foot Headquarters]], [[TCRI Building]], [[Illegitimate Business]], [[Dimension X]], [[Mutant Town]].
* **Deckbuilding Use:** Prioritize on-color duals in two-color decks; they improve consistency and smooth curving. A high percentage of boosters contain one of these gainland-style duals, so you can usually expect to see multiple duals across a sealed pool.

### 10.2. Supplemental Fixing
* **[[Escape Tunnel]]** - True fixing early, unblockable utility late.
* **[[Omni-Cheese Pizza]]** - Good one-shot splash fixing with low opportunity cost.
* **[[Frog Butler]]** - Useful but fragile fixing; do not overcount it as a stable source.
* **[[Everything Pizza]]** - Multicolor payoff that doubles as early fixing (basic land to hand) and a powerful late-game mana sink when your deck naturally supports all five colors.

### 10.3. Splash Discipline
* **Best Splash Style:** Single high-impact off-color card with at least 2-3 credible fixing sources.
* **Deckbuilding Caution:** Do not rely on Food/artifact pieces for color access if your deck also needs those pieces for sacrifice/disappear lines.

## 11. High-Impact Tactical Notes

### 11.1. Block Against Sneak
* **Core Principle:** Prevent unblocked attackers whenever possible; denying Sneak setup often matters more than preserving a low-value creature.
* **Key Threat Example:** [[Shredder, Unrelenting]] is much more punishing when it enters via Sneak and immediately pressures combat.
* **Removal Timing Note:** Use removal before declare blockers when possible; waiting too long gives Sneak decks windows to convert attackers into new threats or to save their creature by Sneaking it back to hand.

### 11.2. Trigger Disappear Immediately
* **Core Principle:** For cards like [[Pizza Face, Gastromancer]] and [[Lord Dregg, Insect Invader]], prioritize lines that guarantee at least one immediate end-step trigger.

### 11.3. Build Around Repeated Triggers
* **Alliance:** Repeat ETBs every turn rather than one large delayed play.
* **Mutagen:** Convert extra counters into real resources (cards, evasion, or lethal pressure), not just larger stats.

### 11.4. Common Legendary Saturation
* **Core Principle:** Because several commons are legendary, avoid too many duplicate copies unless the floor case is still acceptable in your specific build.

### 11.5. Koya, Death from Above Timing
* **Core Principle:** You must pay {3}{B} at the beginning of the *next* end step—the end step of the turn Koya entered. You cannot wait until your following turn to pay. Plan your mana accordingly.

## 12. Cards to Avoid / Traps

**Context-Dependent Warnings:** These cards often underperform. Play them only if your pool is weak or they fill a critical hole.

### 12.1. [[Foot Mystic]]
* **Issue:** Four mana for a 2/4 lifelink. The Disappear trigger (make a 1/1 Ninja) only fires if a permanent left the battlefield under your control this turn. Without that trigger, the card is poor. When you can't enable it reliably, you're paying four for an inefficient body.

### 12.2. [[Putrid Pals]]
* **Issue:** Same pattern as [[Foot Mystic]]. Disappear gives it two +1/+1 counters only if a permanent left this turn. As a 4-mana 3/3 deathtouch without the trigger, it's weak. The format's commons and uncommons are powerful—these cards feel especially bad in comparison.

### 12.3. [[East Wind Avatar]]
* **Issue:** 4-mana 2/4 flying vigilance. Alliance gives it +1/+0 when another creature enters. If it were always a 4-power flyer it would be fine, but often it's cast late and stays a 2/4 or 3/4 most turns. Requires consistent creature flow to justify.
