# MTG Avatar: The Last Airbender - Limited Guide Context

## 0. Format Truths & Characteristics

This guide provides context for building optimal 40-card decks in the *Avatar: The Last Airbender* limited format.

### 0.1. Power Level Distribution
* **Uncommons > Commons**: The power level drops significantly at common. Uncommons are where much of the format's power resides.
* **Prince Format (Bomb-Driven)**: This is a **Prince** format. Many of the rares and mythics are significantly stronger than the rest of the card pool and will often decide games by themselves.
* **Synergy Still Matters**: Synergies (Lessons, Allies, Shrines, Bending payoffs, etc.) are still important, but **raw card quality at rare/mythic is king**. Start with your bombs and the mana needed to support them, then layer synergies on top.

### 0.2. Go-Wide Format
* **White-Containing Decks**: Decks with white (GW, RW, BW, UW) generally want to go wide.
* **Removal Evaluation**: In a go-wide world, balance defensive speed (bodies) with removal for high-priority threats.

### 0.3. Mana Base Considerations
* **Format Archetypes**: Nominally designed around two-color decks (Section 4) and well-supported mono-color decks (Section 4.11), but in practice many of the best decks are **2–3 color bases with additional splashes** for bombs.
* **Hybrid Mana & Secret Gold Cards**: Matter from pack one.
* **50% Dual Lands + Abundant Fixing**: The land slot has a 50% chance to be a dual land, and Green plus certain artifacts/lands provide additional fixing. **This density of fixing supports multi-colored “good stuff” decks** that can reliably cast off-color bombs.
    * **Premium Fixers to Prioritize When You Have Bombs:** [[Cycle of Renewal]], [[Shared Roots]], [[Bender's Waterskin]], [[Barrels of Blasting Jelly]], and even [[Rumble Arena]] (scrying to find lands/colors) all meaningfully improve your ability to cast powerful rares and mythics. Treat these as high picks in packs where you already have bombs or expect to move into a multicolor shell.
* **Tapped Lands & Tempo**: Duals still always enter tapped. Only play duals if your deck actually wants *both* colors or if they are a critical piece of your splash plan. The tempo cost is real, but in a Prince format the upside of casting your bombs often outweighs that cost.
* **Splashing & 3–5 Color Decks**: 2-color decks with a light splash are still a baseline, but with enough fixing you can move into **full 3+ color builds** (especially Green-based shells) to support multiple high-powered rares and mythics. See Section 11.
* **Bomb Evaluation Priority**: **Always evaluate your bomb rares/mythics first.** They should strongly influence your color choices and splashes. See Section 11.1.

### 0.4. Type Tracking
* **Card Knowledge is Key**: Lessons, Allies, or Shrines get slight bumps due to prevalent synergies.
* **Lessons Matter**: Lesson synergies (like on [[First-Time Flyer]] and [[Accumulate Wisdom]]) are more viable in sealed.

### 0.5. Bending Mechanics Power Assessment
All four are stronger than they appear:
* **Airbending**: Extremely flexible. Resets earthbended lands, counters aura removal, and provides tempo. Skill-testing.
* **Waterbending**: Strong mana sink, works well at instant speed. Fits control/tempo.
* **Earthbending**: Very skill-testing (go wide vs. go tall). Earthbending curve-outs are very strong.
* **Firebending**: The finickiest. Requires good attacks and mana sinks. Snowballs when it works but doesn't affect the board directly.

### 0.6. Core Format Philosophy & Strategic Principles

**Be Proactive and Interactive:** This is a fast, tempo-oriented format. Your deck needs to be able to present threats and exert pressure, while also being capable of interacting with your opponent's board.

**Embrace Adaptability:** Cards and decks that can effectively switch between offensive and defensive roles are highly valuable. Being too committed to a single game plan (like an all-out aggressive strategy) can be easily punished, especially when on the draw or facing strong opponent plays.

**Prepare for Bombs:** The format features many powerful rares, mythics, and even uncommons that can win games on their own. If your deck can't apply pressure, you're more likely to lose to these impactful cards. However, well-constructed common and uncommon decks can still compete.

**Understand Creature Stat Lines:**
* Creatures with 2/1, 1/3, and 2/2 stat lines ideally should have an upside (like generating a Clue, providing card selection, or enabling synergies) to justify their inclusion, as their base stats alone are often insufficient.
* Creatures with 2/4 stats are excellent as they are resilient to common removal.
* Two-mana 2/3s and three-mana 2/3s are particularly strong.

**Value Engines and "Cheat" Cards:** Prioritize cards that provide ongoing value over turns or allow you to gain advantages that bypass traditional combat, as these can help you pull ahead or close out games against powerful threats.

**Card Quality Over Synergy (Usually):** Because this is a **Prince** format with extremely strong rares and mythics, **raw card quality (especially bombs) usually trumps synergy**. Synergistic common/uncommon shells are still powerful, but when in doubt between a synergy piece and a stand-alone high-powered card—take the card with the higher ceiling, especially if your fixing can support it.

**Avoid Pure Control:** Building a deck solely focused on being reactive or controlling the game is generally not viable. The format lacks cheap, unconditional counterspells, making it difficult to consistently answer every threat your opponent plays.

### 0.7. Prince Format, Fixing, and Multicolor “Good Stuff” Decks

* **Bomb-Centric Deckbuilding:** Start every sealed pool or draft by laying out your rares and mythics. Assume that if a bomb is castable in the mid-game, it will often decide the game. Your primary goal is to build a shell that can reliably cast your best cards.
* **Multicolor as a Feature, Not a Bug:** The combination of powerful bombs and abundant fixing means that **multi-colored decks are not only viable but often correct**. Don’t be afraid to play three or more colors if your mana base supports it.
* **Fixing Before Greed:** You can only be as greedy as your fixing allows. Prioritize lands and cards that fix your mana (especially in Green and in the land slot) early in the draft when you already have or expect premium bombs. In sealed, explicitly count your fixers before adding each additional color.
* **Card Quality is King:** When a high-powered rare or mythic is in contention with a solid but replaceable role-player, **take the bomb** and build your mana base to support it. This is true in both sealed and draft.

## 1. Format Overview & Key Mechanics

### 1.1. Airbending (White)

* **Function:** Exile target nonland permanent. Its owner may cast it from exile by paying {2}.

* **Strategic Use:**
    * **Aggressive (Tempo):** Remove a key blocker. The {2} mana and turn to replay is a major tempo gain.
    * **Defensive:** Remove a key attacker.
    * **Value (Offensive):** Target your *own* creature with a powerful ETB (e.g., [[Cat-Gator]]) to reuse its effect.
    * **Value (Defensive):** Target your *own* permanent that is targeted by removal (especially Auras like [[Path to Redemption]]) to save it.
    * **Earthbending Interaction:** Can reset earthbended lands by removing the counters.

* **Deckbuilding Priority:** High. **Note:** Airbending makes aura-based removal potentially worse.

* **Risk vs. Reward Evaluation for Airbending:**
    * The {2} mana bounce punishes expensive permanents without immediate board impact (ETBs) but this is not a golden rule.
    * **Prioritize Raw Power:** Always include bombs or high-synergy archetype-definers. Their upside outweighs the risk.
    * **Use This as a Tie-Breaker:** When choosing between replaceable similar cards, prefer the one with an ETB over a "vanilla" creature.

### 1.2. Waterbending (Blue)

* **Function:** Activated ability. When paying the cost, you may tap any number of untapped creatures and/or artifacts you control. Each permanent tapped pays for {1} of the cost.

* **Strategic Use:**
    * Value is card-dependent.
    * **Synergies:**
        * **Go-Wide:** Token makers (e.g., [[Kyoshi Warriors]], [[Forecasting Fortune Teller]]) provide "mana."
        * **Defensive Decks:** Fits control/tempo decks that leave blockers up. Use untapped creatures as "mana" on the opponent's end step.
        * **Vigilance:** Premium, as creatures can attack and then pay waterbend costs (e.g., [[Iguana Parrot]]).

* **Deckbuilding Priority:** High. Blue wants to go wide and play a long game using waterbending as a mana sink.

### 1.3. Earthbending (Green)

* **Function:** `Earthbend X`: Target land you control becomes a 0/0 creature with haste that's still a land. Put X +1/+1 counters on it. If it dies or is exiled, return it to the battlefield tapped (no longer a creature).

* **CRITICAL CLARIFICATION:** Earthbending **CONVERTS** an existing land you control into a 0/0 creature (that's still a land) and puts X +1/+1 counters on it. It does **NOT** create a new creature.

* **Strategic Use:**
    * **Value:** The "death-shield" (returning to play) removes the major risk of turning lands into creatures.
    * **Aggression:** Haste allows for surprise attackers.
    * **Stacking Earthbending:** You can target an already earthbent land to add more +1/+1 counters (go tall).
    * **Attacking and Mana:** **Important:** If you attack with an earthbent land, you cannot tap it for mana (unless it has vigilance). This is a key decision point.
    * **Go Wide vs Go Tall:** A skill-testing decision. Both are valid.
    * **Removal Interaction:** Opponents must eventually use removal on these lands. [[Lost Days]] deals with them permanently.
    * **Synergy with [[Badgermole Cub]]:** [[Badgermole Cub]] synergizes particularly well with earthbent lands, as it benefits from the +1/+1 counters placed on them.

* **Deckbuilding Priority:** High. Provides strong, resilient board presence.

### 1.4. Firebending (Red)

* **Function:** `Firebending X`: When this creature attacks, you get X {R}. This mana lasts until the end of combat (empties at the second main phase).

* **Strategic Use:**
    * **Aggression:** Only works when attacking; belongs in aggressive decks.
    * **Mana Sink:** Mana must be spent during combat. Prioritize:
        1.  **Combat Tricks:** (e.g., [[Cunning Maneuver]], [[Zuko's Conviction]]). This is the primary use.
        2.  **Activated Abilities:** (e.g., on [[Zuko, Exiled Prince]]).
        3.  **Other Instants:** (e.g., sacrificing a Clue).
    * **Bluffing:** Floating mana can make opponents afraid to block.

* **Deckbuilding Priority:** Requires decks with 1-2 mana combat tricks and other instants.

### 1.5. Lessons

* **Function:** A spell subtype (on instants and sorceries).

* **Strategic Use:**
    * **"Lesson-fall":** Some cards get bonuses if a Lesson is in your graveyard (e.g., [[First-Time Flyer]]).
    * **Synergy:** In decks that care about lessons, prioritize playable Lessons (e.g., [[It'll Quench Ya!]], [[Airbending Lesson]]) rather than just blasting redundant copies of cheap protection spells you want to keep in your hand.
    * **Graveyard Synergies:** Some cards scale with multiple lessons (e.g., [[Accumulate Wisdom]], [[Combustion Technique]]).

* **Deckbuilding Priority:** Do not play *bad* Lessons. If you have 2+ "Lesson-fall" payoffs, playable Lessons become higher priority. (Aim for 5-6+).

## 2. Best Commons (By Color)

High-priority cards, ranked by power and versatility. (Card text omitted, strategic value retained).

### 2.1. White

1.  **[[Airbending Lesson]]:** Powerful tempo spell. Bouncing an opponent's creature (forcing the {2} cost) or saving/reusing your own is high value. It replaces itself (draws a card) *and* is a Lesson. Extremely flexible and card-advantage positive. Prioritize highly.
2.  **[[Kyoshi Warriors]]:** Creates two bodies (and two Allies) on one card. Excellent for go-wide, sacrifice fodder, and paying for waterbend costs. Great rate.
3.  **[[Glider Kids]]:** Good stats (2/3 flyer for 2), good types (Human Pilot Ally), and provides value (scry 1). Can grow with ally synergies.
4.  **[[Path to Redemption]]:** Cheap, efficient removal. **Important:** Aura-based removal is weaker in this format. Opponents can airbend their creature away, or still use it for sacrifice/waterbending. Solid, but has downsides.
5.  **[[Razor Rings]]:** Excellent 2-mana instant combat trick/removal hybrid.

### 2.2. Blue

1.  **[[Forecasting Fortune Teller]]:** For 2 mana, gives a 1/3 body and a Clue. Provides two permanents for waterbending (creature + Clue) and replaces itself. Excellent synergy and defensive speed.
2.  **[[Lost Days]]:** Expensive (5 mana) instant removal, but it replaces itself (Clue) and is a Lesson. Effectively deals with earthbended lands (they don't return if put into library). "Second from top" option can be backbreaking.

### 2.3. Red

1.  **[[Firebending Lesson]]:** Highly versatile. A cheap (1 mana) instant-speed removal/trick that's also a Lesson. Can be kicked for 5 mana to deal 5 damage. Premium.
2.  **[[Lightning Strike]]:** Premium common removal (2 mana, 3 damage).
3.  **[[Treetop Freedom Fighters]]:** Two mana for two bodies (2/1 haste Ally + 1/1 Ally). Synergistic, good types, and good early pressure.

### 2.4. Green

1.  **[[Rocky Rebuke]]:** Two-mana instant-speed "fight" spell (deals power, but creature doesn't take damage). Premium green removal.
2.  **[[Ostrich-Horse]]:** Flexible 3-drop. Mills 3, can find a land or become a 4/2.
3.  **[[Rebellious Captives]]:** Solid 2-mana 2/2 Ally. Strong late-game relevance with its 'exhaust' ability to pump itself and earthbend 2.

### 2.5. Black

1.  **[[Sold Out]]:** 4-mana instant removal that requires the creature was dealt damage. Replaces itself with a Clue. Good for turning a chump block into card advantage.
2.  **[[Callous Inspector]]:** Excellent 1-mana 1/1 menace. Synergy piece for sacrifice decks, +1/+1 counter decks, and aggro. Replaces itself with a Clue on death (at cost of 1 life). Sleeper potential.
3.  **[[Corrupt Court Official]]:** 1-mana 1/1 that forces a discard. Good early pressure and late-game disruption.
4.  **[[Deadly Precision]]:** Flexible removal that costs 1 mana + a sacrifice (creature/artifact) OR 5 mana total. Fits perfectly in sacrifice decks.

### 2.6. Colorless/Artifacts

1.  **[[Kyoshi Battle Fan]]:** Premium common. Two mana for a 2/1 (equipped 1/1 token). It's an Ally, provides two permanents for waterbending/sacrifice, and enables "power 4" synergies. Extremely "gluey" card.

## 3. Sleeper Commons

Potentially strong cards that are easy to overlook.

1.  **[[Compassionate Healer]]:** Excellent 1-mana 2/2. Tapping (e.g., for waterbending) to gain life and scry 1 is great for smoothing draws in UW. It's also an Ally.
2.  **[[Wandering Musicians]]:** 3-mana (Boros hybrid) 2/5 Ally. The attack trigger (buffs team +1/+0) is very good for GW and RW go-wide decks.

6.  **[[Aang's Journey]]:** Excellent fixing and lesson synergy.
7.  **[[Pretending Poxbearers]]:** 1-mana (Orzhov hybrid) 2/1 Ally. Death trigger creates another 1/1 Ally. Bread and butter for BW sacrifice decks.

## 4. Key Sealed Archetypes

### 4.1. GW (Green-White): Go-Wide Allies

* **Strategy:** "Go-wide" deck that floods the board with Allies, then uses mass pump effects. **Seems very strong and well-supported.**
* **Gameplan:** Deploy multi-body cards, then use "lord" effects for a game-winning attack.
* **Key Enablers (Multiple Bodies):** [[Invasion Reinforcements]], [[Kyoshi Warriors]] (Allies); [[Badgermole Cub]], [[Haru, Hidden Talent]], [[The Boulder, Ready to Rumble]] (Earthbend).
* **Key Payoffs (Buffs/Synergies):** [[White Lotus Reinforcements]], [[Earth Kingdom Soldier]], [[Invasion Tactics]], [[Team Avatar]], [[Water Tribe Captain]] (Mass Buffs); [[Suki, Kyoshi Warrior]], [[Allies at Last]] (Ally Payoffs).
* **Key Synergies:**
    * **[[Suki, Kyoshi Warrior]]:** A growing threat that creates more Allies on attack, fueling all archetype synergies. Note: It lacks an immediate ETB and must survive a turn (risk vs. Airbending).
    * **[[Haru, Hidden Talent]]:** Key enabler. Every subsequent Ally also earthbends 1, growing your land-creature.

### 4.2. RW (Red-White): Go-Wide Aggro

* **Strategy:** Aggressive "go-wide" deck. Leverages `Airbending` (clear blockers) and `Firebending` (attack safely, generate mana).
* **Gameplan:** Build wide, use `Airbending` on the best blocker, attack with `Firebending` creatures, use the generated mana for combat tricks.
* **Key Gold Cards:**
    * **[[Sun Warriors]]:** Perfect fit. Firebends for X (number of creatures), and its activated ability makes more creatures, creating a powerful loop.
    * **[[Jet, Freedom Fighter]]:** Hybrid mana. An absurdly strong curve-topper. The ETB removal scales with your go-wide board, and the death trigger buffs your remaining creatures.
    * **[[Team Avatar]]:** Makes any lone attacker huge based on your board width. Also a flexible removal spell via its discard ability.
* **Key Enablers:** [[Kyoshi Warriors]] (W); [[The Cave of Two Lovers]], [[Treetop Freedom Fighters]], [[Fire Nation Attacks]] (R).

### 4.3. UW (Blue-White): Waterbending Control

* **Strategy:** **Important:** This is a heavy-blue *Waterbending Control* deck, not a flyers tempo deck. The flyers theme is at odds with Blue's primary goal (go wide for waterbending mana sinks). Focus on a long, controlling game.
* **Gameplan:** Put lots of permanents (creatures, artifacts, tokens) on board. Use waterbending as an end-step mana sink. Hold up mana for counterspells.
* **Key Cards:** [[Momo, Playful Pet]], [[Iguana Parrot]] (vigilance is key), [[Compassionate Healer]], [[Boomerang Basics]], [[Invasion Submersible]], `Airbend` cards, [[It'll Quench Ya!]], [[Aang, the Last Airbender]], [[Glider Staff]], [[Sokka, Lateral Strategist]], [[Cat-Owl]].
* **Key Synergies:**
    * **[[Air Nomad Legacy]]:** Anthem for your flyers that also creates a Clue (another permanent for waterbending).
    * **[[Momo, Playful Pet]]:** Excellent 1-drop (flying, vigilance). The "leaves the battlefield" trigger works with `Airbending` your own Momo for value.
    * **[[Teo, Spirited Glider]]:** Provides card selection (loot) when your flyers attack.

### 4.4. BW (Black-White): Sacrifice

* **Strategy:** Classic "aristocrats" deck. Wins by sacrificing disposable permanents (creatures, tokens, Clues) for value. Needs balance of "Fodder," "Outlets," and "Payoffs."
* **Gameplan:** Generate Fodder, establish a Sacrifice Outlet, win via drain Payoffs or by building an unbeatable threat.
* **Key Cards:**
    * **Outlets:** [[Hei Bai, Spirit of Balance]], [[June, Bounty Hunter]], [[Joo Dee, One of Many]], [[Deadly Precision]].
    * **Payoffs:** [[Tolls of War]], [[Zhao, Ruthless Admiral]], [[Jet's Brainwashing]] (steal-and-sac), [[Buzzard-Wasp Colony]].
    * **Fodder:** [[Corrupt Court Official]], [[Callous Inspector]], [[Momo, Playful Pet]], Ally tokens, Clue tokens, [[Fire Navy Trebuchet]] token, [[Kyoshi Battle Fan]].
* **Key Synergies:**
    * **[[Tolls of War]]:** Key engine (Hidden Stockpile variant). Creates a Clue, then makes a 1/1 Ally *once per turn* when you sacrifice a permanent.
    * **[[Hei Bai, Spirit of Balance]]:** Sacrifice outlet that grows, then moves its counters to another creature when it leaves play.
    * **[[June, Bounty Hunter]]:** Turns your creatures into Clues, providing more sacrifice fodder and card draw.

### 4.5. UB (Blue-Black): Draw Two Control

* **Strategy:** **Crucially, this is a control deck first** and a "draw two" deck second. It wins via 1-for-1 trades and long-game card advantage. The "draw two" triggers are a bonus.
* **Gameplan:** Survive the early game with cheap removal/blockers. Pull ahead with card draw (Clues) and card selection. Trigger "draw two" synergies incidentally.
* **Key Early Game (Control):** [[Watery Grasp]], [[Forecasting Fortune Teller]] (makes a Clue), [[It'll Quench Ya!]].
* **Key Payoffs (Draw Two):** [[Foggy Swamp Spirit Keeper]] (Gold), [[Knowledge Seeker]], **[[Messenger Hawk]]** (great card in the blue-black archetype), **[[Raven Eagle]]** (excellent rare in the blue-black archetype). **Important:** Clue tokens are super valuable in this format, providing both card advantage and enabling "draw two" synergies.
* **Enablers:** [[June, Bounty Hunter]] (can make Clues).
* **Key Synergies:**
    * **[[Foggy Swamp Spirit Keeper]]:** 3-mana 2/4 lifelinker. Creates 1/1s when you draw your second card *each turn* (can trigger on your turn and opponent's turn).
    * **[[Knowledge Seeker]]:** Good 2-drop. A growing threat that replaces itself with a Clue on death, which then enables future "draw two" triggers.
    * **[[Messenger Hawk]]:** Excellent in the blue-black archetype. The flying body provides early defense and pressure, while the "draw two" trigger synergizes perfectly with the deck's game plan. Clue tokens are extremely valuable in this format, making cards that create them high priority.
    * **[[Raven Eagle]]:** Excellent rare in the blue-black archetype. Its ability to keep giving clue tokens is incredibly powerful, providing a steady stream of card advantage and enabling "draw two" synergies turn after turn. The repeatable clue generation makes it a key engine for the deck.
    * **[[Hama, the Bloodbender]]:** A powerful top-end control finisher that steals spells from the opponent's graveyard.

### 4.6. UR (Blue-Red): Lessons & Noncreature Spells

* **Strategy:** A "spells matter" tempo deck that aims to fill the graveyard with noncreature spells (especially Lessons) to power up creatures. **Aim for at least 6 playable Lessons.**
* **Concern:** This archetype seems weak. It doesn't support Blue's waterbending plan, and Red wants to be more straightforwardly aggressive.
* **Gameplan:** Play "payoff" creatures, then use cheap instants/Lessons to control combat, protect threats, and fuel the graveyard.
* **Key Payoffs (Lesson-Grave):** [[First-Time Flyer]], [[Accumulate Wisdom]], **[[Combustion Technique]]** (premium removal that scales with Lessons in grave).
* **Key Payoffs (Noncreature):** [[Dragonfly Swarm]] (Gold), [[Serpent of the Pass]], [[Firebending Student]].
* **Key Synergies:**
    * **[[Dragonfly Swarm]] (Gold):** A flyer whose power scales with noncreature spells in grave. Replaces itself if a Lesson is in grave when it dies. Risk: Can be outclassed by Green's large creatures.
    * **[[Serpent of the Pass]]:** Top-end threat. Cost is reduced by noncreature spells in grave, and can be cast with Flash.
* **Good Lessons:** [[Iroh's Demonstration]] (a flexible board wipe or removal spell; can be a "savage beating").

### 4.7. UG (Blue-Green): Ramp & Lessons

* **Strategy:** A ramp deck using Green's acceleration to play big threats and Lessons for value. **This deck can easily splash a third color** for bombs due to Green's fixing.
* **Gameplan:** Use ramp spells, use Lessons/card draw to find threats, cast powerful, expensive bombs.
* **Key Mana Ramp (Acceleration):**
    * **[[Hermitic Herbalist]] (Gold):** Excellent 2-drop (2/3). Provides ramp and fixing, and adds *two* mana for Lesson spells.
    * [[Raucous Audience]], [[Shared Roots]] (Lesson), [[Cycle of Renewal]] (Lesson), [[Bender's Waterskin]] (artifact), [[Barrels of Blasting Jelly]] (artifact).
* **Key Payoffs (Ramp Into):**
    * **[[Bumi, King of Three Trials]]:** Top-end threat (6-mana 7/7) that also provides card selection (scrybend 3) if you have Lessons in grave.
    * [[Flopsie, Bumi's Buddy]], [[Badgermole]], [[Saber-Tooth Moose-Lion]], any Bombs (e.g., [[The Legend of Kyoshi]], [[Bumi, Unleashed]]).
* **Mana Base Note:** When splashing, state clearly if it's a 3-color build or a 2-color (UG) base with a splash. In this Prince format, **UG is one of the best shells for 3–5 color “good stuff” decks** because of its natural access to ramp and fixing—lean into this when your bombs pull you in multiple colors.
* **Gold:** **[[Guru Pathik]]** (Simic hybrid): Powerful 2-mana 2/4. ETB finds a Lesson/Saga/Shrine. Buffs creatures when you cast those spells. Fits in many blue/green decks.

### 4.8. BR (Black-Red): Firebending Aggro

* **Strategy:** Highly aggressive deck centered on `Firebending`. **Must have a low, fast curve (lots of 2-drops)** to attack immediately. Needs combat tricks and removal to push damage.
* **Gameplan:** Play cheap firebenders, attack, use generated mana for instants (tricks/removal) to clear blockers.
* **Key Firebenders:** [[Fire Nation Cadets]], [[Fire Sages]], [[Tundra Tank]] (Vehicle), **[[Azula, On the Hunt]]** (4-drop, firebend 2, makes a Clue), **[[Zuko, Exiled Prince]]** (high-risk/reward).
* **Key Instants (Payoffs):** [[Fire Nation Attacks]], [[Sold Out]], [[Zuko's Conviction]], [[Cunning Maneuver]].
* **Note on [[Fire Nation Attacks]]:** An instant that makes two firebending tokens. Can be cast during combat using mana from other firebenders. The 9-mana flashback is also achievable with firebending mana.

### 4.9. BG (Black-Green): +1/+1 Counters & Earthbend

* **Strategy:** Midrange deck using +1/+1 counters, primarily from `Earthbend`. **Key Insight: Do not over-prioritize synergy.** Draft the best cards on raw power, and the synergies will happen.
* **Gameplan:** Play efficient creatures, use `Earthbend` to create large threats, benefit from minor counter payoffs.
* **Key Cards (Payoffs):** [[Dai Li Agents]] (Gold), [[Earth Kingdom General]], [[Buzzard-Wasp Colony]], [[Hog-Monkey]], [[Toph, the Blind Bandit]], [[Badgermole Cub]].

### 4.10. RG (Red-Green): Power 4+ Matters

* **Strategy:** Deck that cares about creatures with power 4+. Combines `Earthbend` (Green), `Firebending` (Red), and "4+ power" payoffs. **Note:** This archetype looks more green than red.
* **Gameplan:** Get 4+ power creatures into play, use payoffs that reward you for attacking with them.
* **Key Gold Cards:**
    * **[[Bitter Work]]:** 3-mana enchantment. Draws a card when you attack with a 4+ power creature. Its 'exhaust' ability can earthbend 4, creating a 4/4 haste creature to trigger itself immediately.
    * **[[Earth Rumble Wrestlers]]:** A 2-mana 4/3 if you control another 4+ power creature.
    * **[[Flopsie, Bumi's Buddy]]:** Finisher. ETB puts a +1/+1 counter on your whole team, then gives your 4+ power creatures pseudo-menace.
    * **[[Uncle Iroh]]:** `Firebending` and Lessons payoff.
* **Key Bombs:** [[Bumi, Unleashed]], [[Toph, Hardheaded Teacher]].

### 4.11. Mono Color

* **Strategy:** Commits to a single color (or a light splash, e.g., 13 Plains / 4 Islands) to unlock powerful mono-color-matters cards.
* **Key Payoffs:** Each color has a payoff that scales with its basic land type, but they have different requirements:
    * **White:** [[Gather the White Lotus]] (makes Allies based on Plains) - **Requires super heavy white commitment** (13+ Plains) to be effective. Only build around this if you're going nearly mono-white.
    * **Blue:** [[Waterbending Scroll]] (draws cards based on Islands) - **Works well in 2-color blue decks** with at least 10 Islands. Can be a solid payoff even when splashing a second color.
    * **Black:** [[Cat-Gator]] (deals damage/lifelink based on Swamps) - **Works well in 2-color black decks** with at least 10 Swamps. The ETB scales nicely even with moderate black commitment, making it viable in decks like BW or UB.
    * **Red:** [[Solstice Revelations]] (card advantage based on Mountains) - **Requires super heavy red commitment** (13+ Mountains) to be effective. Only build around this if you're going nearly mono-red.
    * **Green:** [[Rockalanche]] (makes huge creature based on Forests) - **Requires super heavy green commitment** (13+ Forests) to be effective. Only build around this if you're going nearly mono-green.
* **Format Insight:** You can often build 2-color *archetypes* (like UW Flyers) with a *mana base* that is primarily one color with a moderate splash of the second, maintaining consistency while accessing synergies. However, given the Prince nature of this format and the abundance of fixing, **dedicated mono-color decks are less common than multi-color bomb/fixing decks** unless your bombs and payoffs strongly reward staying mono-colored. **Note:** [[Cat-Gator]] and [[Waterbending Scroll]] are exceptions—they can provide meaningful value even in 2-color decks with 10+ of their respective basic land type.

### 4.12. 5-Color Shrines

* **Strategy:** A niche "for fun" archetype, not a top-tier strategy. Built around "Shrine" enchantments (all are legendary).
* **Note:** This is very hard to build. All Shrines cost 4 mana. It's a late-format dream, not a reliable strategy.
* **Key Cards:** [[Aang's Journey]] (finds Shrines/lands), [[Guru Pathik]] (finds Shrines), [[White Lotus Hideout]] (helps cast Shrines), [[Kyoshi Island Plaza]] (Shrine that fixes).

6.  **[[Flopsie, Bumi's Buddy]]:** 6-mana 4/4 finisher. ETB pumps your whole team with +1/+1 counters, then makes your big (4+ power) creatures harder to block.

2. **[[Cycle of Renewal]] / [[Shared Roots]]:** Lesson-based ramp/fixing that both accelerates you and helps you find the colors you’re missing. Excellent in any green deck, and especially in UG “5-color good stuff” shells.
3. **[[Rumble Arena]]:** Primarily a land that scries 1 on ETB—this smoothing is very real in multicolor decks, helping you hit both land drops and colors. The vigilance text only matters once the land is earthbent.
4. **[[Barrels of Blasting Jelly]]:** Starts as fixing for your splashes, but its real strength is as **incredible repeatable removal** once set up—one of the best reasons to be base-green multicolor.

## 6. Notable Omissions (Cards That Look Good But Have Issues)

**Context-Dependent Warnings, Not Blacklists:** If your pool is weak, you can play these to fill holes.

### 6.1. [[Path to Redemption]]
Aura-based removal is significantly worse in this format due to `Airbending`. Opponents can airbend their enchanted creature, and your aura falls off. It's going to be borderline unplayable in some matchups. [[Airbending Lesson]] is much higher priority.

### 6.2. [[Rowdy Snowballers]]
This type of "Frost Lynx" effect (3-mana 2/2 that taps/stuns) has not been powerful enough in modern limited formats.

### 6.3. [[Swampsnare Trap]]
This is an aura, not hard removal. **It leaves the permanent on the battlefield**, which is a huge liability. The opponent can still:
1.  `Airbend` it (fizzling your aura)
2.  `Waterbend` with it
3.  `Sacrifice` it
Materials on the battlefield are valuable in this format. Evaluate this as "sorcery-speed, destroy target creature with toughness 3 or less," which is much weaker.

## 9. Combos You Can Assemble

### 9.1. Energybending + Mono-Color Payoffs
[[Energybending]] (which turns all your lands into all basic land types) combos with the mono-color payoff cards (e.g., [[Gather the White Lotus]], [[Cat-Gator]]) to maximize their "number of Plains/Swamps" effect.

### 9.2. Airbending Lesson + Sagas
With a Saga (e.g., [[The Cave of Two Lovers]]), you can respond to the third chapter's trigger by `Airbending` your *own* Saga. You get the full effect, then can replay the Saga for value.

### 9.3. Zuko's Conviction + Land Cycling Creatures
Use landcycling creatures (e.g., [[Canyon Crawler]]) to get a large creature into the graveyard, then use the kicker on [[Zuko's Conviction]] to reanimate it at instant speed.

### 9.4. Jet's Brainwashing + Deadly Precision
Use the kicker on [[Jet's Brainwashing]] to steal an opponent's creature, attack with it, then sacrifice it to [[Deadly Precision]] (which requires a sacrifice) to kill another creature. A powerful steal-and-sac combo.

### 9.5. Airbending + Cat-Gator
This is a key value combo. `Airbend` your own [[Cat-Gator]] to reuse its powerful ETB.

### 9.6. Watery Grasp + Lost Days
When you activate [[Watery Grasp]]'s waterbend ability, it forces the opponent to shuffle their library. This disrupts any card they might have placed second from the top with [[Lost Days]], making the combo particularly effective at denying your opponent's setup plays.

### 9.7. The Cave of Two Lovers + Secret Tunnel
[[The Cave of Two Lovers]] has a powerful interaction with cave cards like [[Secret Tunnel]]. On its second step, the saga searches your library for a Mountain or Cave card (such as Secret Tunnel) and puts it into your hand. You can then play Secret Tunnel as a land. On the third step, when the saga earthbends 3, you can target Secret Tunnel. Secret Tunnel has "This land can't be blocked," so when it becomes a creature via earthbending, it becomes an unblockable 3/3 haste creature (0/0 base with three +1/+1 counters). This creates a devastating combo where you search for Secret Tunnel, play it, then earthbend it to get an unblockable attacker. Such a good combo!

### 9.8. Barrels of Blasting Jelly + Toph, the First Metalbender
[[Barrels of Blasting Jelly]] pulls double duty as premium fixing *and* one of the best removal engines in the format. With [[Toph, the First Metalbender]] in particular, you can **earthbend the Barrels into a creature, sacrifice it, and then immediately get it back**, repeating this loop to machine-gun down most creatures in the format over multiple turns. If you have Toph and Barrels in the same pool, you should strongly consider building your deck and mana base to support this interaction.

## 10. Final Tips & Format Notes

### 10.1. Mana Sinks Abound
The format is full of mana sinks (Bending abilities, activated abilities, sacrifice-to-draw dual lands).
* **Terminology Note:** When writing strategy primers, distinguish:
    * **Mana ramp** (acceleration): Getting more mana (e.g., [[Hermitic Herbalist]]).
    * **Mana fixing** (color consistency): Getting the right colors (e.g., dual lands, [[Ostrich-Horse]]).
    * **Card advantage** (card draw/selection): Getting more/better cards (e.g., Clues, [[Waterbending Lesson]]).
* **Implications:**
    * You don't need to invest as heavily in card draw spells, as mana sinks provide late-game value.
    * **Do not shave lands.** You want to hit 17 lands to fuel these sinks.
    * Aggro decks won't run out of gas as easily, so control decks need their own finishers.

### 10.2. Hand Management Against Black Decks
Keep an extra, unneeded land in your hand in the late game to protect your important spells from [[Corrupt Court Official]].

### 10.3. Format Characteristics
* **Late Format Potential**: Hidden synergies exist (Lessons, Shrines).
* **All Mechanics Are Strong**: Don't underestimate any of the four Bending mechanics.
* **Go-Wide Aggro**: Aggro decks go wide, forcing control decks to have defensive speed, not just removal.
* **Format Depth**: All 10 color pairs are supported.

## 11. General Deckbuilding Principles

1.  **Evaluate Bomb Rares & Mythics First:** Before committing to colors, identify all rares and mythics in the card pool. These are format-defining cards that can win games on their own. Strongly consider building your deck around these colors.

2.  **Leaning Into Multicolor for Bombs:** When evaluating bombs, you will often find powerful cards outside your main two colors. In this Prince format, the combination of **absurdly strong rares/mythics and abundant fixing (50% dual lands, Green's fixing, artifacts)** means that building multi-color decks to support your best cards is often correct.
    * **When Splashing / Going 3+ Colors Is Correct:**
        * **The Card:** It is a true bomb that can single-handedly win games. Do *not* stretch your mana for merely "good" cards.
        * **The Fixing:** You have at least **three reliable sources** of each splash color (preferably more if you are adding multiple off-colors).
        * **The Cost:** The card ideally has a single colored pip. Double/triple pips are still much harder to support unless that color becomes a main color.
        * **The Deck:** Your deck is midrange/control leaning and can afford some tempo hits from tapped lands and slower starts.
    * **Strategy Primer Clarity:** If you include extra colors, clearly state whether you are a **2-color base with one or more splashes** or a **full 3–5 color “good stuff” deck**. Both are viable when your fixing and bombs justify it.

3.  **Respect Tempo:** `Airbending` makes tempo a key factor. Do not rely on expensive, do-nothing permanents.

4.  **Count Your Lessons:** If building "Lesson-fall" cards (like [[First-Time Flyer]]) or the UR archetype, you *must* include a sufficient number (at least 5-6) of *playable* Lessons. In sealed, you're more likely to have access to lessons than in draft.

5.  **Enable Your Mechanics:**
    * **Firebending (BR/RW):** Requires a high density of cheap instants/tricks.
    * **Waterbending (Blue):** Requires a good number of creatures/artifacts to tap. Go-wide cards are high-priority.
    * **Earthbending (Green):** Value cards with this mechanic highly. Earthbending curve-outs can be very strong.

6.  **Interaction:** [[Airbending Lesson]] (White) and [[It'll Quench Ya!]] (Blue) are premium common interaction. Prioritize them. Be extremely picky about your removal in a go-wide world.

7.  **Play Around Counterspells:**
    * **[[It'll Quench Ya!]]** (2 mana): Expect it early. Be wary of playing your best 3-drop into 2 open Blue mana.
    * **[[Sokka's Haiku]]** (5 mana): If the opponent passes with 5 mana open, be cautious. Try to bait it out with a less important spell before casting your bomb.

8.  **Defensive Speed Matters**: In a go-wide format, you need a balance of defensive speed (bodies on the battlefield that can hold back multiple attackers) plus removal to pick off the really important threats.

9.  **Type Tracking**: Be aware of types. Cards that are Lessons, Allies, or Shrines get slight bumps because of how prevalent these synergies are. Track these in your deckbuilding.

10. **Mana Base Considerations**: From the start, think about hybrid mana, monocolor payoffs, and secret gold cards. **Terminology:** When writing strategy primers, be precise: "mana ramp" = acceleration (more mana), "mana fixing" = color consistency (right colors but something with a cost), "card advantage" = card draw/selection (more cards).

## 12. Card-by-Card Evaluation Framework

**CRITICAL:** When building a deck, you must evaluate each card in the card pool not just by its raw power, but by understanding:
1. **Which archetypes/decks this card wants to fit into**
2. **How good the card is within those archetypes using Quadrant Theory**

This comprehensive evaluation will help you make better deckbuilding decisions and understand the true value of each card in your specific pool.

### 12.1. Archetype Identification

For each card in the pool, identify which archetypes it fits into:

* **Color-Based Archetypes:** Which color pairs can play this card?
* **Mechanic-Based Archetypes:** Does it synergize with Airbending, Waterbending, Earthbending, Firebending, or Lessons?
* **Strategy-Based Archetypes:** Does it fit go-wide, control, aggro, midrange, or combo strategies?
* **Type-Based Archetypes:** Is it an Ally, Lesson, Shrine, or other relevant type?

**Example Analysis:**
* **[[Kyoshi Warriors]]** fits into:
    * GW Go-Wide Allies (creates two Allies, enables mass buffs)
    * BW Sacrifice (two bodies for sacrifice fodder)
    * UW Waterbending Control (two permanents to tap for waterbending)
    * RW Go-Wide Aggro (two attackers for aggressive strategies)
    * Any white deck that wants board presence

### 12.2. Quadrant Theory Evaluation

For each card, evaluate how it performs in the four game states (Quadrant Theory):

#### **Quadrant 1: Developing (Early Game - Building Your Board)**

* **Question:** How good is this card when you're setting up your board in the early game?
* **Consider:**
    * Does it affect the board immediately?
    * Does it provide value over time?
    * Is it a good curve play?
    * Does it help you establish your game plan?

* **Examples:**
    * **[[Kyoshi Warriors]]:** Excellent in developing—4 mana for two bodies is great rate, establishes board presence
    * **[[Forecasting Fortune Teller]]:** Good in developing—provides a body and a Clue, sets up waterbending
    * **[[Firebending Lesson]]:** Excellent in developing—cheap removal that can answer early threats

#### **Quadrant 2: Parity (Mid-Game - Even Board State)**

* **Question:** How good is this card when the board is even and both players are trading resources?
* **Consider:**
    * Does it break parity by providing card advantage?
    * Does it create favorable trades?
    * Does it generate value that pulls you ahead?
    * Is it efficient enough to matter in a close game?

* **Examples:**
    * **[[Airbending Lesson]]:** Excellent in parity—removes a threat, draws a card, creates tempo advantage
    * **[[Waterbending Lesson]]:** Excellent in parity—draws three cards, massive card advantage
    * **[[Invasion Reinforcements]]:** Good in parity—flash means you can ambush attackers, creates two bodies

#### **Quadrant 3: Ahead (Winning - Maintaining Your Lead)**

* **Question:** How good is this card when you're winning and need to close out the game?
* **Consider:**
    * Does it help you win faster?
    * Does it prevent your opponent from coming back?
    * Does it provide inevitability?
    * Is it a finisher or threat?

* **Examples:**
    * **[[Team Avatar]]:** Excellent when ahead—makes all your creatures huge threats, closes games quickly
    * **[[Firebending Lesson]]:** Good when ahead—can finish off opponent or remove blockers
    * **[[Toph, the Blind Bandit]]:** Excellent when ahead—creates a large threat that's hard to answer

#### **Quadrant 4: Behind (Losing - Need to Catch Up)**

* **Question:** How good is this card when you're losing and need to stabilize?
* **Consider:**
    * Does it affect the board immediately?
    * Does it provide card advantage to catch up?
    * Can it answer multiple threats?
    * Does it buy you time?

* **Examples:**
    * **[[Iroh's Demonstration]]:** Excellent when behind—can wipe opponent's board of small creatures
    * **[[Airbending Lesson]]:** Good when behind—removes a threat, draws a card, buys time
    * **[[Lost Days]]:** Good when behind—deals with a threat, creates a Clue, but expensive

### 12.3. Archetype-Specific Quadrant Evaluation

For each card, evaluate its quadrant performance **within each archetype it fits into**:

**Example: [[Compassionate Healer]] in UW Waterbending Control**

* **Developing:** Excellent—1 mana 2/2 is great rate, provides early board presence
* **Parity:** Excellent—taps for waterbending, provides card selection (scry), gains life
* **Ahead:** Good—can tap for waterbending while attacking, provides value
* **Behind:** Good—blocks well, provides life gain and card selection to stabilize

**Overall:** This card is excellent in UW Waterbending Control because it performs well in all quadrants, especially in parity and when behind.

**Example: [[Firebending Lesson]] in BR Firebending Aggro**

* **Developing:** Excellent—cheap removal, answers early blockers
* **Parity:** Good—efficient removal, but doesn't provide card advantage
* **Ahead:** Excellent—removes blockers, helps close games
* **Behind:** Mediocre—only deals with one threat, doesn't provide card advantage or board presence

**Overall:** This card is excellent in BR Firebending Aggro because it's crucial for developing and maintaining your lead, even though it's weaker when behind.

### 12.4. Decision-Making Framework

When deciding which cards to include in your deck:

1. **Identify Archetype Fit:** Which archetypes can this card go into based on your pool?

2. **Evaluate Quadrant Performance:** How does this card perform in each quadrant within those archetypes?

3. **Consider Your Deck's Needs:**
    - Does your deck need more cards for developing? (Early game threats, curve plays)
    - Does your deck need more cards for parity? (Card advantage, value engines)
    - Does your deck need more cards when ahead? (Finishers, threats)
    - Does your deck need more cards when behind? (Removal, board wipes, card draw)

4. **Compare to Alternatives:** When choosing between similar cards, consider:
    - Which has better quadrant coverage?
    - Which fits your archetype better?
    - Which addresses your deck's weaknesses?

5. **Raw Power as Starting Point:** Use your read of each card's raw power as a baseline, but don't rely on it exclusively. A card with lower raw power might be better for your specific deck if it:
    - Fits your archetype perfectly
    - Performs well in the quadrants your deck struggles with
    - Has synergies with your other cards

### 12.5. Common Evaluation Patterns

**Cards That Excel in Multiple Quadrants (High Priority):**
* **[[Airbending Lesson]]:** Good in all quadrants—tempo, card advantage, removal, value
* **[[Aang, the Last Airbender]]:** Excellent in all quadrants—threat, removal, value engine
* **[[Invasion Reinforcements]]:** Good in all quadrants—flash, two bodies, instant speed

**Cards That Are Archetype-Specific:**
* **[[Firebending Lesson]]:** Excellent in BR/RW Firebending Aggro, but weaker in other archetypes
* **[[Waterbending Lesson]]:** Excellent in blue decks with waterbending, but unplayable in non-blue decks
* **[[Earthbending Lesson]]:** Excellent in green decks with earthbending synergies, but weaker elsewhere

**Cards That Are Quadrant-Specific:**
* **[[Iroh's Demonstration]]:** Excellent when behind (board wipe), good when ahead (removal), weaker in developing/parity
* **[[Team Avatar]]:** Excellent when ahead (finisher), good in parity (threat), weaker when behind
* **[[Forecasting Fortune Teller]]:** Excellent in developing/parity (value engine), weaker when ahead/behind

### 12.6. Practical Application

When building your deck:

1. **Start with your bombs and high-tier cards**—these are usually good in multiple quadrants
2. **Fill in your curve**—prioritize cards that are good in developing
3. **Add value engines**—prioritize cards that are good in parity
4. **Include finishers**—prioritize cards that are good when ahead
5. **Add interaction**—prioritize cards that are good when behind
6. **Balance your quadrant coverage**—make sure you have answers for all game states

**Remember:** A card's raw power is its general strength, but understanding its archetype fit and quadrant performance tells you its value **in your specific deck**. A weaker card that fits perfectly into your archetype and covers your deck's weaknesses is often better than a stronger card that doesn't fit your strategy. When building a focused, synergistic deck (e.g., Allies, Sacrifice, Lessons), a lower-tier card that is essential to that synergy is always more valuable than a higher-tier "good stuff" card that doesn't fit the plan.
