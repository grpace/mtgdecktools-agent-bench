### **Supplemental Context: "Secrets of Strixhaven" Limited Format**

This deck is for **Secrets of Strixhaven (SOS)** limited (sealed or draft). Use this together with the main deck-building instructions in the prompt.

**How the deck builder uses this context:** Your **Available Card Pool** lists every card you may play. This section explains SOS format, colleges, and card roles—it does **not** add cards to the pool. Before finalizing a 40, you must run the prompt’s **comparative analysis** (compare 2–3 viable strategies), then build only from cards in the pool. Card names below (e.g. [[Snarl Song]]) are **examples** of roles and patterns; include them only if they appear in the pool with enough copies.

Five **colleges** map to five enemy-color pairs; each has a signature mechanic and a clear deck identity.

**Mystical Archive:** Packs include an extra Archive card legal for the deck. The sheet is instant/sorcery heavy (more interaction and splash temptations). Build your main college plan first; treat Archive uncommons as real construction factors, and rare/mythic Archive hits as bonuses, not guarantees.

**Format:** Lots of **enemy-color gold** at common and uncommon. Start from a stable two-color base, then add a third color when fixing and payoffs justify it. Use overlap packages and converge where they fit; avoid five-color soup without a plan.

**Synergy vs power:** A small set of commons matters a lot. Loose synergy often loses to clean curves of strong spells, creatures, and interaction. Either stack **redundant** pieces in the **open** college (twos, enablers, payoffs that line up every game) or take high-floor cards and interaction first and treat mechanics as overlap. If the pool hinges on one fragile combo turn, favor cards that still work when synergy misses.

**Pace and polarization:** The format rewards two broad plans. **Aggressive white** (Silverquill pressure, strong white rares) punishes slow starts. **Multicolor control** (Prismari, Quandrix, or Lorehold with converge splashes) stabilizes, then wins with converge payoffs and top-end spells. Most games still go medium-long, but you must pick which side of that fight your 40 is on.

**Converge in control shells:** Community consensus treats **converge payoffs as a package inside control**, not a standalone five-color identity. Fixing plus interaction first; then [[Snarl Song]], [[Arcane Omens]], and friends. [[Snarl Song]] is often the **real win condition**—one big turn that ends the game—and it gets better when the deck can **recur it from the graveyard** (flashback, yard card flow like [[Pursue the Past]], or other reuse). These lists run **few creatures on purpose**—sometimes **fewer than eight**—because bodies become easy removal targets and do not end games. A lean creature count plus strong spells and converge finishers is normal and correct. **Control mirrors** are often decided by who has the bigger top end ([[Snarl Song]], [[Together as One]], [[Improvisation Capstone]], etc.)—plan to go over the top, not win on creature damage. Multicolor control is a top-tier plan when mana is coherent.

**What keeps control in check:** Fast **white** decks with premium rares ([[Repel Calamity]], [[Practiced Offense]], [[Stirring Hopesinger]], Silverquill gold) pressure before converge decks assemble. **Lorehold (RW)** is the other pillar: flashback and yard value let it **grind** with control while still pressuring early—do not assume every long game favors UR/UG converge.

**Format tempo:** Medium speed leaning slow—control and converge mirrors go long—but you still need early board impact and removal. Giving proactive white a free start is punishing.

**Table/meta snapshot (shifts with pod):** **Lorehold** and **Silverquill** lead on consistency; Lorehold especially grinds well. **Prismari/Quandrix control + converge** is a proven high-end strategy when fixing and payoffs show up. **Witherbloom** is often the weakest college at crowded tables unless open and built aggressive. There is **no real Rakdos (BR) college lane**—do not force black-red as a primary identity; splash black removal into RW or UR/UG control when the pool supports it instead.

**Color tie-breakers (loose):** White and red lead on proactive power (Silverquill, Lorehold, Prismari), then black, blue, then green—green is frequently an **enabler** (fixing, ramp, splash) for multicolor converge shells rather than the deepest standalone common pool.

**Draft navigation matters more than static "college recipes":** In SOS, knowing baseline college identities is only part of winning. Big edges come from in-draft adjustments: identifying whether your seat/deck is becoming aggro vs control, deciding when to splash, and balancing interaction/card draw/finishers based on your actual pool.

**Top-end:** Seven-mana finishers are real deckbuilding priorities in this environment. Cards like [[Improvisation Capstone]] are not only for dedicated ramp shells; any deck with solid early defense and coherent mana can play one or two true top-end cards to break stalls.

**Card flow:** Prepare, flashback, and graveyard loops add extra cards, so one-for-one trades matter less. Plan for repeated mana use and late turns where whoever spends mana better pulls ahead.

**Prepare in lists:** Prepare adds spell count without cutting creatures—good for shells that cast many instants and sorceries.

### **Building a 40 from Your Pool (Sealed or Draft)**

Follow this order so the final list matches the prompt’s required comparative analysis:

1. **Scan the pool by card quality and color.** The strongest cards are bombs and build-arounds *when castable* in your colors. Synergy can justify a weaker card that fits the plan; do not play weak “signposts” just because they match a college name.
2. **Compare 2–3 strategies** (e.g. Silverquill pressure vs Lorehold grind vs multicolor control with converge). For each option, mentally list creatures, removal, curve, and mana—pick the plan with the best mix of **power, curve, interaction, and mana consistency**, not just the highest single card.
3. **Choose a primary two-color college** (enemy pair) with the most castable gold and on-color playables. Add a third color or converge package only when fixing in the pool supports it ([[Environmental Scientist]], [[Terramorphic Expanse]], [[Potioner's Trove]], college duals).
4. **Hit composition targets** (adjust for plan): **Aggro / pressure** (Silverquill, aggressive Witherbloom): about **15–17 creatures**, solid removal, **17–18 lands**. **Multicolor control + converge** (Prismari, Quandrix, control Lorehold): **very low creature count is fine**—often **6–10 creatures**, sometimes fewer if the pool supports it—with heavy interaction, fixing, and multiple ways to **go over the top** in control mirrors. Do not add fair creatures that die to removal; add finishers and card flow instead. Either shell wants **at least five answers** to opposing threats and enough fixing to cast gold and splashes.
5. **Mystical Archive:** Treat the Archive card as a real slot—interaction, finisher, or splash enabler—not a free excuse to add colors you cannot cast.
6. **Final cuts:** Remove cards that only work on a perfect synergy turn if the pool has higher-tier playable alternatives in the same slot.

### **Key Card Roles (only if in your pool)**

**Finishers:** Seven-drops and rare closers ([[Together as One]], [[Improvisation Capstone]], [[Practiced Offense]], [[Ark of Hunger]], etc.)—add mana and defense to cast them, not filler instead of removal.

**Converge (control win conditions):** In control shells, [[Snarl Song]] is often the deck’s main finisher—prioritize casting it on a winning turn and recurring it if the pool has graveyard tools. [[Arcane Omens]], [[Together as One]], and Archaic finishers supplement; they are not substitutes for interaction and fixing.

**Mana:** Fixing and ramp ([[Environmental Scientist]], [[Terramorphic Expanse]], [[Potioner's Trove]], college duals, [[Paradox Surveyor]]) enable gold and splash; two fixing effects is a minimum in two-color decks.

**Interaction:** Premium answers ([[Repel Calamity]], [[Silverquill Charm]], [[Last Gasp]], [[Essence Scatter]], [[Grapple with Death]], etc.)—keep over medium creatures when the deck is short on answers.

**Often cut unless the pool heavily supports them:** [[Witherbloom, the Balancer]], [[Tam, Observant Sequencer]], [[Pigment Wrangler]], [[Pest Mascot]], [[Comforting Counsel]], [[Steal the Show]], college “engine” legends that need a full synergy package ([[Silverquill, the Disputant]], [[Prismari, the Inspiration]]).

### **Key Set Mechanics**

1. **Prepare:** Creatures may have an inset prepare spell. While prepared, you may cast a copy; that unprepares the creature. Prepared does **not** stack. Prepare increases spell count without cutting creatures (strong for repartee/opus). Many prepared bodies are fragile—cash the spell when you can before a clean removal window. Re-prepare effects are premium. Judge each card on whether the **creature is good enough if you never untap**.
2. **Repartee (Silverquill — WB):** Triggers when you cast an instant or sorcery that **targets** a creature. Run enough real targeting spells for payoffs; do not run bad tricks only for triggers. Best Silverquill is low-curve pressure plus removal and combat tricks that already target. Curve and pressure usually matter more than “finding” triggers.
3. **Opus (Prismari — UR):** Triggers on instant/sorcery casts; **five+ mana spent** upgrades the reward. Default build supports big spell turns, not only cheap prowess chains. Pick **one** lane before final cuts: big Opus (ramp/fixing, survive to five mana) or low-curve prowess-like (cheap spells, early pressure). Mixed builds fail often. Reward ramp and fixing that cast expensive spells a turn early; in slower Opus, you can hold a payoff creature until you can pair it with a 5+ mana spell the same turn.
4. **Infusion (Witherbloom — BG):** Checks if you **gained life this turn** (any amount). Trigger it off combat, Pests, and ETB value; do not spend whole cards only to gain life. High life totals make life-payment cards easier. **With [[Potioner's Trove]]:** cast an infusion spell, hold priority, activate Trove for life, then let the spell resolve so infusion sees the life gain.
5. **Flashback (Lorehold — RW):** Casting from the yard exiles the spell. Lorehold cares about cards **leaving** the graveyard—flashback is both value and triggers. Play as midrange value, not pure aggro. Cheap graveyard exits help double-spell with payoffs. If the deck needs narrow graveyard density, draft **redundant** enablers; thin packages collapse when one piece is missing.
6. **Increment (Quandrix — GU):** Triggers when mana spent to cast a spell is greater than the creature’s power or toughness. Counters slow increment as creatures grow—use X-spells and big mana turns. Can be ramp/value or counters-tempo. Easier to keep online than fragile graveyard piles if mana is coherent; still avoid +1/+1 synergy without enough mana-heavy turns.
7. **Paradigm:** Some **Lesson** sorceries have paradigm: first time one resolves, you exile it, then each first main you may cast a **copy** for free. **Learn** is not in SOS; Lesson is for older-card synergy.
8. **Converge:** Payoffs scale with colors of mana spent. **Best home: control shells** (Prismari UR, Quandrix GU, or Lorehold RW splashing) with **3–4 colors** and strong fixing—not five-color soup. [[Snarl Song]] is the headline payoff and often ends games outright; build so you can cast it reliably and **get extra copies from the yard** when possible (flashback, rummage-and-flashback lines, etc.). Add [[Arcane Omens]], [[Together as One]], or Archaic closers after mana and removal are solid. Prismari-plus-splash and GU-plus-fixing are the usual converge carriers; green is often fixing, not the whole plan.
9. **Book:** Artifact subtype on some cards; matters only where typal or text references it.
10. **Ward / protection:** Ward and clunky removal reward proactive play. Cheap tricks that fit your plan help; pure delay that does not touch board often underperforms.

### **Draft Archetypes (College Lanes)**

* **Silverquill (WB) — Repartee pressure:** Castable gold curve and real targeting spells. [[Imperious Inkmage]] and [[Render Speechless]] are the defensive spine; [[Inkling Mascot]] and [[Quill-Blade Laureate]] carry repartee triggers on castable bodies. [[Conciliator's Duelist]] can target itself to blink and rebuy ETBs with targeting spells. [[Scolding Administrator]] and [[Snooping Page]] punish slow starts—answer them with early interaction in other decks. [[Silverquill Charm]] is flexible removal and combat. [[Lecturing Scornmage]] and [[Melancholic Poet]] are curve fillers. Cut [[Social Snub]] and [[Silverquill, the Disputant]] unless sacrifice or narrow synergy is already there. Pattern: early pressure, real removal ([[Erode]], charms), [[Cost of Brilliance]] when the deck is controlling. When Lorehold is overdrafted, Silverquill’s proactive curve is often the more reliable lane.

* **Prismari (UR) — Pick one shell:** **Big Opus / spells control (default):** Survive, then chain 5+ mana spells. Run **few creatures**—enough to block or trade early, not a full midrange creature suite. Center on [[Stress Dream]], [[Vibrant Outburst]], [[Sanar, Unfinished Genius]], [[Divergent Equation]], and [[Tablet of Discovery]]; cut filler bodies for interaction and converge. [[Prismari Charm]] and [[Impractical Joke]] stabilize; [[Subterranean Tremors]] and [[Traumatic Critique]] close games. [[Elemental Mascot]] and [[Stadium Tidalmage]] only if spell count is already high. **Prowess-like (situational):** more creatures and cheap spells—only with a clear low curve. Do not mix shells. Natural converge partner when fixing appears ([[Snarl Song]], [[Arcane Omens]], [[Together as One]]).

* **Witherbloom (BG) — Infusion (often weak when contested; strong when open):** Build **life-gain aggro**, not generic "BG out-grinds everyone." [[Blech, Loafing Pest]] and [[Essenceknit Scholar]] pressure; [[Thornfist Striker]] and [[Pestbrood Sloth]] turn life gain into damage or board. [[Bogwater Lumaret]] is early curve into Pests. [[Grapple with Death]] answers big threats. [[Moseo, Vein's New Dean]] is a postcombat payoff when infusion is already happening—do not build the whole deck to enable it alone. [[Lluwen, Exchange Student]] loops cheap Pest prepares from the graveyard. Cut [[Pest Mascot]] and [[Witherbloom, the Balancer]] unless the pool is already deep in infusion enablers. **Versus Lorehold**, take the beatdown role—RW yard value punishes slow BG.

* **Lorehold (RW) — Flashback midrange / grind (top-tier):** One of the format’s best lanes: pressure early, then **out-resource** opponents. [[Pursue the Past]] rummages and flashbacks for card advantage—hold extra lands for burst turns; it also helps **stock and replay** big spells including converge finishers. [[Rubble Rouser]] rummages, ramps, and sets up flashback; [[Molten Note]] removes early and flashes back later. [[Wilt in the Heat]] and [[Startled Relic Sloth]] stabilize. [[Kirol, History Buff]] and [[Spirit Mascot]] support yard triggers when density is high. [[Stone Docent]] and [[Ark of Hunger]] scale in long games. With fixing, splash converge ([[Snarl Song]], [[Arcane Omens]]) and black removal ([[End of the Hunt]])—Lorehold plus converge is a strong control hybrid, not pure aggro.

* **Quandrix (GU) — Multicolor control anchor:** Default plan is **control + converge**, not fragile increment gimmicks. Keep the **creature count low**; prioritize fixing, permission, and converge over a board of medium creatures. [[Environmental Scientist]] and fixing rocks/duals enable [[Snarl Song]] and splashes. [[Paradox Surveyor]], [[Embrace the Paradox]], and [[Proctor's Gaze]] smooth mana and tempo. Cut [[Fractal Mascot]] and [[Pterafractyl]] for interaction when increment is thin. Treat [[Snarl Song]] as a finisher, not a side plan.

### **Practical Build Rules**

* **Removal:** Many creatures snowball; rares often must die. Sometimes burn early engines ([[Scolding Administrator]], [[Snooping Page]], [[Cuboid Colony]]) instead of hoarding removal for a bigger threat. Drawback removal is situational, not automatic four-of.
* **Cheap interaction:** Prefer spells that answer engines on curve ([[Impractical Joke]], [[Last Gasp]], [[Essence Scatter]]). Blank tempo plays need a plan to convert.
* **Curve:** Strong 2–3–4 in base colors matters; gold carries a lot of power. Avoid stacking fours and fives unless twos and defense are great.
* **Mana:** Do not run unrelated enemy two-drops without great fixing. Anchor one enemy pair, then splash.
* **Fixing benchmark:** Prioritize about two mana-fixing lands/effects in most two-color decks ([[Environmental Scientist]], [[Terramorphic Expanse]], [[Potioner's Trove]], college duals); converge decks want more. SOS has many two-mana gold cards, so consistently casting both colors on turn two matters more than usual.
* **Final cuts:** Prefer cards that affect the board, answer threats, or end games over narrow synergy pieces that only matter on a perfect sequence. Keep role-specific answers (e.g. [[Grapple with Death]] in BG) over generic filler.
* **Reading the pool for a college:** Count strong gold and on-color cards for each enemy pair (WB, UR, BG, RW, GU). The pair with the most strong castable playables is usually your base; do not spread gold across three colleges without fixing.
* **Splashes:** College duals ([[Forum of Amity]], [[Spectacle Summit]], [[Titan's Grave]], [[Fields of Strife]], [[Paradox Gardens]]), [[Terramorphic Expanse]], [[Strixhaven Skycoach]], [[Paradox Surveyor]] support a coherent third color—not random greed.
* **Grindy splash rule:** In long games, late premium off-color removal is often worth a light splash. If packs 2-3 offer cards like [[End of the Hunt]] or [[Wander Off]], even modest fixing (for example one [[Terramorphic Expanse]] plus one [[Potioner's Trove]]) often supports a single splash basic because you usually see enough cards before you must cast it.
* **Lands:** Many sinks (prepare, flashback, surveil lands, X-spells). Top-heavy decks with fixing often want **18** lands; Opus-on-curve decks lean that way more than usual.
* **[[Potioner's Trove]] role:** Treat [[Potioner's Trove]] as premium structural glue, not only a converge card. Any-color mana fixes converge splashes (even colors you have **no** basics for) and the “gain 2 if you cast an instant or sorcery” line stabilizes against Silverquill reach and Prismari burn so you reach big turns.
* **[[Pursue the Past]] (Lorehold):** Hold disposable lands when you can; casting plus flashback in one turn is a real burst of cards, and the discard naturally stocks the graveyard for flashback synergies.
* **Charms:** Respect two-mana combat. [[Prismari Charm]] and [[Silverquill Charm]] are premium; [[Witherbloom Charm]] and [[Lorehold Charm]] are narrower but real.
* **Blue:** Leaving mana up with [[Essence Scatter]], [[Run Behind]], [[Muse's Encouragement]] punishes bad attacks—build instant-speed density if you play that game.
* **Multicolor control (default converge shell):** Stabilize with cheap interaction and fixing (often UR or GU base, sometimes RW splashing). **Very low creature counts are normal**—even under eight is acceptable when removal and finishers are strong. Win **control mirrors** by going over the top: extra removal, card advantage, then converge or seven-mana bombs—not by out-creaturing the opponent.
* **Blue macro-plan:** In Prismari/Quandrix control, prioritize interaction, fixing, then **finishers that end mirror games** ([[Snarl Song]], [[Together as One]], [[Subterranean Tremors]], [[Improvisation Capstone]]). Cut medium creatures before cutting a top-end threat.
* **White as the format check:** Aggressive white rares and Silverquill gold punish slow control mana. If building converge control, you need early plays and removal for white-based pressure—not only top-end bombs.
* **Lorehold grind:** RW can match control in long games; do not overload converge payoffs at the expense of flashback value and early creatures when the pool supports Lorehold.
* **Prepare and combat:** Do not protect prepared creatures so hard you skip good attacks or blocks. Board still comes first.
* **Prismari final check:** Cut toward one lane—half prowess / half big spell loses games.
* **Archive:** Cut flashy Archive rares/mythics unless they fit role and mana. Keep stabilizers, finishers, and clean interaction.

### **Common Limited Traps**

* **Repartee overbuild:** Weak tricks just for triggers tank the deck. Play targeting spells you would run anyway.
* **Conflicting gold:** Too many early gold cards from different pairs yields uncastable hands.
* **Prepare traps:** Low-impact bodies with slow prepare ([[Tam, Observant Sequencer]]); filler [[Biblioplex Tomekeeper]] without strong prepare targets. Worst case: expensive prepared threats like [[Pigment Wrangler]] that need untap + held spell + double target—cut if the body is not fine alone.
* **Converge without a control shell:** Slapping [[Snarl Song]] into a midrange pile without fixing, interaction, and early plays loses to white pressure. Converge belongs in **multicolor control**—not as an excuse to skip removal for payoffs.
* **Undervaluing [[Snarl Song]] recursion:** If the pool has graveyard tools, treat [[Snarl Song]] as a repeatable finisher, not a one-shot splash.
* **Too many creatures in control:** Padding a converge control 40 with a full creature suite is a common mistake—fair bodies eat removal and rarely close games or control mirrors. Prefer interaction, fixing, and **multiple top-end threats** over the eighth or ninth medium creature.
* **Greedy top-end without defense:** Seven-drops are strong in SOS, but they still need a shell that can survive. If your twos, interaction, and fixing are weak, adding more expensive bombs usually worsens the deck instead of improving inevitability.
* **Witherbloom positioning:** BG is often the weakest lane at balanced tables; avoid forcing it. Enter when it is clearly open and focus on redundant early pressure plus natural life-gain payoffs, not fragile grind engines. **Conflict watch:** Intuition says BG should outscale Lorehold in long games; in SOS, RW flashback/value often steals that role—slanting aggressive is frequently correct.
* **Converge deckbuilding:** Successful converge decks are **control-first** (UR, GU, or RW plus splash)—not Rakdos, not five-color soup. Fix mana, answer threats, then win with [[Snarl Song]] and similar payoffs.
* **White split:** White feeds both Silverquill and Lorehold—mixing both without payoffs yields muddled priorities.
* **[[Borrowed Knowledge]]:** High variance; often wrong unless the deck supports it.
* **Narrow build-arounds:** [[Comforting Counsel]], [[Flashback]] (the card), [[Fix What's Broken]], [[Zombify]], and spell-copy traps need dense support or they rot in the main deck.
* **College signpost traps:** [[Witherbloom, the Balancer]], [[Tam, Observant Sequencer]], [[Pigment Wrangler]], [[Silverquill, the Disputant]], and [[Prismari, the Inspiration]] read like engines but rarely carry a 40-card list—play only with exceptional support, not as the deck’s plan.
* **Prismari mascot trap:** [[Elemental Mascot]] and [[Stadium Tidalmage]] are not auto-plays in UR; prefer [[Stress Dream]], [[Sanar, Unfinished Genius]], and [[Divergent Equation]] unless spell count is already high.
* **Fractal / increment filler:** [[Fractal Mascot]], [[Fractalize]], and [[Pterafractyl]] need frequent big spell turns—cut for [[Environmental Scientist]] or interaction when increment is thin.
* **Five-drop pile:** Many “fine” fives exist—too many clogs the deck. Keep fives that stabilize or power your engine.
* **Low-impact tempo:** Tap/bounce that does not change combat or life totals underperforms unless the deck is real tempo.
