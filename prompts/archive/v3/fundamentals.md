## Limited fundamentals (MTG Deck Tools)

_General guidance every player gets, whatever the set. These are principles, not rules: use judgement. Set-specific advice, when you have it, comes from the set primer and overrides anything here._

### Drafting
- **Goal:** a 40-card deck of about 23 playables (usually two colours) plus 17 lands. Every pick should serve that deck.
- **Read the cards.** Read each card's actual text, cost and stats before judging it. Win-rate numbers, when shown, are useful context, not answers.
- **Early picks (roughly pack 1, picks 1–5):** take the most powerful card. Bombs, efficient removal and strong evasive or value creatures come first. Stay flexible; mono-coloured cards keep more options open than gold cards.
- **Reading signals:** good cards of one colour still arriving around picks 5–9 suggest that colour is open upstream. Packs 1 and 3 pass to your left (you're fed from your right); pack 2 passes the other way.
- **Committing:** most drafters settle on two colours by early pack 2. Pivot only for a clear signal or a big power gap. Once committed, a solid on-colour card usually beats a stronger card you won't play.
- **Context matters more than raw card quality:** your colours, your curve (enough two- and three-drops), creature count (about 14–17), removal count, and synergy with the archetype you're building. A lower-rated card that fills a real hole or enables your deck's plan can be the right pick.
- **Gold cards** are excellent in your exact colours and speculative otherwise. Duplicates of key commons in an archetype get better as you collect them.
- **Lands:** basic lands can appear in packs, but every deck gets unlimited basics at deckbuilding. Take a basic only when nothing in the pack could possibly make your deck. Non-basic lands that fix your colours can be worth a mid pick.
- **Late in pack 3**, if nothing helps you, taking a card that would beat you (hate-drafting) is fine.

### Deckbuilding
See the MTG Deck Tools deckbuilding guide at the deckbuilding step: compare two or three builds, curve and land counts, the "one structural flaw" rule, splash land math, hybrid and land rules, interaction and ways to win.

### Playing
- **Mulligans:** keep most 7-card hands with 2–5 lands and plays you can make. Mulligan hands with 0–1 or 6–7 lands unless the rest is exceptional.
- **Mana:** play a land every turn you can, and use your mana efficiently each turn. When paying, tap lands so that what's left untapped can still cast what you may need (e.g. keep the colour for your instant).
- **Know your role:** be the beatdown when your deck is faster or the board favours you; play control when you'll win the long game. Re-evaluate each turn.
- **Combat:**
  - Before attacking or blocking, count both sides' power, toughness, life totals and untapped mana. Open mana can mean a trick or flash.
  - Make attacks the opponent can't block profitably. Don't chump-block unless it buys something real.
  - Every turn, check whether you have lethal and whether they do.
- **Removal and tricks:** save premium removal for real threats when you can afford to. Use tricks when they win combat or save a key creature.
- **Card advantage vs tempo:** two-for-ones win long games; tempo wins races. Know which game you're playing.
