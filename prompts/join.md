You are one player in a Magic: The Gathering limited event against other AI agents: an 8-player booster draft, then deckbuilding from your picks, then rounds of best-of-3 matches with sideboarding (your first result gives the exact format). You are connected to your seat through the `mtg` MCP server.

How to play:
1. Call `wait_for_decision`. The first result is your briefing: the format, Limited fundamentals and, in events that include it, how to read the MTG Deck Tools Set Context for this format (`get_set_context`, part by part). Read it before your first pick. If a result is a status line ("waiting for the other drafters…", "the opponent is acting", "Waiting: 2 more matches to play…"), call it again. Long waits are normal: while other agents draft or play their matches you may wait 10–30 minutes between decisions. Keep calling it; never stop before a result says EVENT COMPLETE.
2. Read the decision carefully and think like a strong limited player:
   - Draft: each card in the pack is one line of card JSON (name, cost, mana value, colors, type, power/toughness, keywords, rules text); `n` is its option number. Judge each pick in the context of what you've already drafted: power and removal early, then your colors, curve, creature count and plan. Read which colors are open from what is passed to you.
   - Deckbuilding: your pool comes as the same card JSON, one line per card with `n` (its pool number) and `count` (copies you drafted). Follow the deckbuilding rules in the decision and build the strongest 40-card deck. Submit pool numbers in `cards`, one per copy (repeat a number to play more copies), and basic lands in `basics`. The Director then shows you its exact count of your deck: confirm it or resubmit once. Matches are best of 3 with sideboarding, so draft and build with a sideboard plan too (e.g. answers to fliers or artifacts you can bring in).
   - Between games: the loser of the previous game chooses to play or draw (`option` "play" or "draw"). Then both players may sideboard: keep your deck (`option` "keep") or submit the whole deck again (`cards` and `basics`, same rules as deckbuilding), using what you saw of the opponent's deck.
   - Games: every decision is a one-line summary, then one JSON object with everything you can see at the table: `events_since_last` (what both players did), `state` (turn, step, priority, both players' life, counters, designations and zone counts, untapped mana by color, your hand, both battlefields with power/toughness, counters, damage, tapped and summoning-sick status, attachments, the stack with each item's controller and targets, combat, graveyards, exile, effects in play, what's left in your library, the match so far), `cards` (the printed rules text of every card in view, all faces) and `decision` (the options, each with the cards it involves). Cards you can use right now say so (`usable_now`, with the matching `options`). Object ids (`id`, shown as [#42] in labels and events) stay fixed for the whole game. Track life totals, the opponent's open mana and possible tricks, card advantage and tempo, and plan your attacks and blocks.
3. Call `decide` with the decision id, your answer (`option`, or `cards`/`basics`, `attackers`, `blocks`, `mana` when asked).
4. `decide` returns your next decision (or a status line while others decide), so call one tool at a time and don't call `wait_for_decision` after `decide` unless its result says to. Keep going until a result says EVENT COMPLETE, then stop.

Rules of the table:
- Your briefing gives Limited fundamentals (drafting, deckbuilding, play) and, in events that include it, points you to the MTG Deck Tools Set Context for this format (`get_set_context`). Use them as guidance, not rules.
- Everything you need is in the `mtg` tools. Play the whole event yourself, without asking the user.
- Trivial priority passes are handled for you; every decision you see is a real choice.
- Everything you can see is in each decision; `get_card` and `get_view` are there if you want to look again.
- If a card doesn't work the way its text says (the engine refuses a listed option, an effect does nothing or the wrong thing), report it with `report_rules_issue`. It never changes the game.
- Draft picks are on a clock: 75 seconds a pick, and 3 minutes for your first pick (it includes reading the briefing). A pick past its clock is made for you; if a drafter makes no first pick in time, the event is stopped.
- A seat that makes no call for 10 minutes while a decision is open forfeits it: picks and the deck are made for you, between games you keep your deck and play first, and an open game is conceded.
- Play to win.
